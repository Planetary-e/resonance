# v0.4 quiet-period policy

Status: decision recorded on 2026-09-29. Updated 2026-09-30: the shared end-to-end deadline and publication, search, and per-mailbox hold controls are implemented and tested. Experimental collection windows and idle-traffic policy are unchanged.

## Decision

Use bounded, opportunistic mixing for ordinary interactive delivery. Add no automatic cover traffic, no idle crowd polling, and no automatic increase in collection windows when traffic is quiet. This mode protects encrypted contents and separates source and operation observations between non-colluding relays; it does not promise to hide the timing of an isolated operation.

For a user who cannot accept that timing exposure, provide an explicit **hold locally** control. An unsent operation stays encrypted on the device until the user releases or cancels it, or its validity expires. A deadline must never silently release it. Holding prevents that operation from being transmitted; it does not certify anonymity when the user eventually sends it. Do not offer an automatic “wait until anonymous” mode based on elapsed time, packet counts, or relay claims.

The resource policy is therefore low overhead with an honest delivery choice. It does not waive the roadmap's outstanding observer-protection requirements or make v0.4 complete. Continuous cover traffic remains a research alternative, not the selected next implementation. No permanent operator-run service or external mixnet dependency is introduced.

## Evidence and alternatives

The [request experiment](../evals/private-mix-2026-09-29T14-09-59-545Z.md) found a benefit for busy request batches. The [reply experiment](../evals/private-reply-2026-09-29T14-30-17-612Z.md) reduced loopback burst reply-order matches from 24/24 to 2/24, but simulated-delay bursts changed from 5/24 to 6/24. Mixed sparse replies remained fully matched in both profiles. Reply batching added 0.73–0.77 seconds to median return latency; full completion p95 reached 5.63 seconds in the delayed burst condition. These are small local rank-attack diagnostics, not Internet anonymity measurements.

The [offline budget calculation](../evals/quiet-period-budgets-2026-09-29.md) uses the captured 15,495-byte request and 11,213-byte reply. It is an arithmetic sensitivity analysis, not another network experiment. For illustrative scheduled exchanges:

| Interval | Client application MB / 24 connected hours | Mean slot wait before network/mixing |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 second | 2,307.571 | 0.5 seconds |
| 10 seconds | 230.757 | 5 seconds |
| 60 seconds | 38.460 | 30 seconds |
| 5 minutes | 7.692 | 150 seconds |

These costs omit transport framing, discovery, handshakes, retries, other relay links, and CPU. Larger size buckets cost more. The wait calculation assumes uniform arrival within fixed slots, one operation per slot, and no backlog. It does not select a cover rate or predict battery consumption.

Waiting for natural traffic also has a limit. Under a hypothetical Poisson model with one other compatible arrival per minute, five seconds provides only an 8.00% chance of another request. At one per hour, fifteen minutes provides 22.12%. Whole-network traffic is not the relevant pool: route, size, and observation window matter. Another packet is not evidence of another independent, honest user.

| Alternative | Benefit it might provide | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Existing short request/reply collection windows | Reordering when compatible traffic overlaps | Keep experimental; no quiet-period escalation |
| Much longer relay queues or timed release | More opportunity for natural overlap | Do not make this the default; more delay alone cannot certify protection and requests expire |
| Wait for a minimum packet, IP, or relay-reported user count | A visible release condition | Reject as an anonymity gate; repeated senders, NAT, Sybils, and operator claims invalidate the inference |
| Prefer a relay advertising a larger crowd | Potentially larger shared batches | Defer; occupancy claims are unverified and selection can concentrate trust or aid an attacker |
| Continuous client cover traffic | Concealing idle/active transitions under an appropriate protocol | Defer until cost, energy, and stronger attack measurements justify it; relay-only noise does not conceal client transmissions |
| Explicit local hold | No transmission of the held operation | Select as a user control; make no promise about a later release |

## Budgets and behavior

These are engineering constraints for implementation and evaluation, not measurements of energy or new production guarantees.

| Area | Chosen constraint |
|---|---|
| Quiet-period traffic while idle | **0 additional bytes** for cover, crowd probes, or privacy keepalives |
| Quiet-period power work while idle | **0 periodic wakeups or network polls** added by this policy; no wake-from-sleep or keep-awake requirement |
| Interactive quiet-specific delay | **0 extra waiting** beyond the existing bounded experimental queues |
| Interactive latency target | Local benchmark p95 ≤ **6 seconds** for the current eight-client workloads; a **10-second overall deadline** includes queueing, discovery, DNS, and all route attempts |
| Local hold | No operation-related DNS, discovery, sockets, token reservation, or automatic network retry until explicit release |
| Hold storage | Each of the publication and request outboxes allows at most **64 entries and 16 MiB of serialized encrypted snapshot data**, whichever is reached first; reject overflow visibly. Combined limits are 128 entries / 32 MiB, excluding atomic-replacement temporary files |
| Hold expiry | Cancel/expire locally, never send or silently renew a signed operation; a future submission requires a valid operation |
| Device-owner limits | Existing owner limits remain authoritative for discretionary relay work; accepted durability obligations retain their existing servicing rules |

The zero-idle budgets apply to additional quiet-period privacy activity, not all application traffic, relay maintenance, or existing durability work. Encryption and local persistence have nonzero CPU/storage costs when the user creates a held operation. No battery percentage is claimed from byte counts. Any future background privacy mode must be separately metered, respect power and network conditions, and measure energy on supported hardware before adoption. Plugging in must not automatically enable cover traffic.

Every private client operation, including `connect()`, now starts one monotonic ten-second budget before request creation and scheduling. The same budget covers entry rotation, destination fallbacks, independent DNS validation, request encryption, transmission, and reply processing. Shorter queue/discovery limits still apply. Expiry removes waiting work, cancels native DNS queries, terminates active client sockets, and prevents further fallbacks or late crypto completions from sending. One operation's expiry does not cancel another. Timers cannot preempt synchronous CPU work or a suspended event loop; elapsed-time checks at stage boundaries reject late continuations when execution resumes.

`PrivateOperationError` exposes timeout/cancellation codes and an outcome of `not-sent` or `unknown`. `not-sent` concerns the operation payload; discovery may already have occurred. Once a payload was handed to the socket, timeout or disconnect cannot prove rejection. Transport uncertainty survives later failed fallbacks. The client does not release or reassign a reserved admission token: resubmitting the same signed publication to the same destination reuses its exact binding. This does not make newly generated mailbox requests identical retries. Relationship mailbox fetch/ack fanout shares one budget and retains replies validated before expiry, as its existing partial-success API permits; success does not certify that every destination answered.

The 6-second p95 is a local regression target, not a promise for an arbitrary Internet route. The preceding measured 5.63-second result leaves little headroom, so add no longer quiet-period windows. The deadline tests establish bounded failure handling, not a new latency or anonymity measurement.

## Implementation boundary and acceptance checks

1. **One operation deadline — implemented.** Unit tests cover queue/jitter time, entry fallbacks, stalled and cancelled DNS, per-operation isolation, and elapsed-time checks before a delayed timer callback. Live socket tests cover stalled destination fallback, shared mailbox fanout budgets with timely partial results, and an accepted publication whose final reply is withheld. The publication remains stored, the client reports an unknown outcome, and an exact retry succeeds with the same reserved wallet token and a duplicate acknowledgement.
2. **Publication hold — implemented separately from the network scheduler.** States are `held`, `sending`, `delivered`, `expired`, `cancelled`, and `outcome-unknown`. `openPublicationOutbox` holds the exact signed publication, pinned private entry/destination URLs, and opaque local application data. At most four releases run concurrently; excess release attempts stay held. Cancellation targets only that publication. There is no background timer, polling, or automatic release. Expiry is processed on local access. Release checks signed validity before creating a client; the private client checks again before discovery, capability reservation, and actual payload transmission. Transport envelopes are created at release, never at hold. Discovery may already have happened when validity expires during a send. Search and mailbox requests instead use the intent policy below because their signed requests are short-lived.
3. **Hold controls — implemented in the desktop private-transport pilot.** Publications, searches, and individual mailbox checks have explicit save/release controls. “Saved on this device; not sent” describes a hold. Ordinary delivery must not display an “anonymous now” badge based on queue size. Holding is an explicit preference; it is not silently activated from guessed network population. Do not require repeated confirmations for every ordinary search.
4. **Measure stronger observers before production timing defaults.** Include real-versus-idle classification, cross-hop linkage, sizes and connection setup/closure, repeated sessions, multiple destinations, and failure/reconnect behavior. Test sparse workloads without supplying the attacker with labelled trial boundaries. Report uncertainty and compare with unchanged controls. Record total transport bytes and CPU; byte arithmetic cannot stand in for power measurements.

Regression checks for implementation must prove zero network activity and zero capability reservation for held work, no release on timeout/restart, bounded storage, cancellation before and after transmission, preservation of destination-accepted state after reply loss, and a single deadline across stalled discovery and fallback paths. The current 30-second private envelope must not be created before a potentially long hold.

## Publication outbox: operation and recovery

Enable the existing pilot with `RESONANCE_EXPERIMENTAL_PRIVATE_ROUTE_URLS=entry,destination[,fallback...]`. The Publish screen offers **Send now** and **Save on this device**. Saving preserves the signed seven-day expiry; time spent holding does not renew it. Each saved item has explicit Send and Cancel controls. An uncertain send offers **Retry same publication**, retains the original signature/route, and never describes cancellation as undoing delivery. Completed entries can be removed manually to free capacity. Removing an expired entry with an earlier unknown outcome does not withdraw any remote copy. A cancelled, definitely unsent item can be removed locally.

`publication-outbox.json` uses AES-256-GCM with a separately derived identity key, authenticated domain, and fresh nonce per snapshot. Limits are 64 entries and 16 MiB for the serialized encrypted snapshot, including local item data. Atomic replacement temporarily needs space for both old and new snapshots; this is not a total disk/RAM cap. The file and temporary snapshots use owner-only permissions. Each replacement flushes the file before renaming and flushes the containing directory on POSIX; Windows lacks the directory-flush step. Capacity refusal preserves earlier entries; an I/O failure makes the open outbox unusable until close/reopen, so delivery cannot proceed based on guessed persistence.

A process lock prevents simultaneous writers. Restart can recover a lock belonging to a confirmed dead process. Malformed locks, a live/reused PID, or an interrupted lock-recovery guard fail closed and require inspection. Pending `sending` entries reopen as `outcome-unknown`, never as unsent and never with an automatic retry. No stored capability is released or reassigned after uncertainty.

A page reload asks for the password again because browser credentials are kept only in memory. The backend verifies that password before issuing a new token even if its identity is already unlocked; old tokens then stop working. Lock preserves the fact that a profile already exists, so the UI offers Unlock rather than Create Identity. Storage-read failures return an API error instead of terminating the desktop server.

The desktop holds the item text, vectors, publication fingerprint, mailbox identifiers, and private keys together inside the encrypted outbox. It inserts nothing into the ordinary item/publication store until an acknowledgement is durably recorded. This prevents the app's normal Matches refresh from polling a held publication's mailbox. After an acknowledgement, local import is idempotent across restart and preserves later withdrawals. The standalone outbox accepts an admission-capability provider; desktop wallet integration remains separate work. Holding one publication does not stop unrelated app traffic, other publications' mailbox sync, or volunteer relay obligations.

Checks cover encrypted-file tampering and wrong keys; count/byte refusal; failed atomic replacement and directory flush; failure before send intent and after acknowledgement; concurrent release; cancellation with definite versus uncertain outcomes; SIGKILL recovery; expiry during the client queue; and receipt-loss recovery through real local relays with an exact reserved-token retry. The desktop HTTP integration exercises hold, lock/unlock, Matches refresh, authentication, Send, Cancel, removal, and local recovery after an acknowledged send. Its vector is fixed to avoid a model download; networking, signatures, storage, and relay handling are real. UI rendering checks cover held/unknown/expired labels and permitted controls. A separate browser check used the real cached matching model and local relays to save, reload/re-authenticate, explicitly send, and cancel a publication. These are single-computer checks, not a multi-device pilot or an anonymity certification.

## Search and mailbox intents: operation and recovery

The Search screen offers **Save search on this device** and later **Run**. Matches offers **Pause automatic checks**, a publication/channel selector, and **Save mailbox check**. Saving a mailbox check atomically persists the pause with the saved intent. Matches, channel lists, and individual-channel refreshes then return local data without contacting mailboxes; the channel screen also stops its five-second refresh timer. An in-flight automatic check is disconnected and guarded against subsequent fetch, acknowledgement, or deposit calls. Already-transmitted work cannot be recalled. Completion, cancellation, removal, expiry, and restart never resume automatic checks; enabling them requires an explicit action and no pending saved mailbox checks.

`private-request-outbox.json` uses the same encrypted atomic snapshot and process-lock mechanism with a separate identity-derived key and authenticated domain. It stores search text/type/fingerprint or one publication/relationship mailbox identifier, the pinned private route, and completed results. It has its own 64-entry / 16 MiB capacity, including retained history. It stores no newly generated signed request or token reservation while held. A saved intent expires **24 hours after saving**, processed on local access with no timer or automatic renewal. Completed results remain until manually removed and can become stale; these are saved results, not live search results.

Run durably records send intent before creating a client. Fresh signed search/fetch/ack requests are generated during that explicit run and retain their protocol lifetimes. At most four runs are active, each with a ten-second overall deadline and a separate cancellable client. A mailbox run selects exactly one mailbox before any fetch or pending-message flush. It includes required acknowledgements, connection responses, and that relationship's pending outgoing messages. It cannot poll or flush unrelated mailboxes. Expired/withdrawn publications and closed channels are rejected before network work. Explicit Connect, Send, Close, publication withdrawal, and volunteer relay obligations remain separate actions; this is not a global offline mode or an independent queue for every outgoing message.

Interrupted `sending` entries reopen as `outcome-unknown`. No retry starts automatically. **Run again (new request)** creates fresh signed requests and may require fresh capability tokens; it never reassigns an earlier uncertain reservation. This differs from a publication's exact retry. Definite pre-send cancellation is labelled unsent; after transport may have started the UI retains uncertainty. The terminal client guard prevents late continuations from sending after Stop, timeout, or session lock. Existing holds retain their pinned route and remain manageable even if new private-pilot configuration is removed on a later restart.

Checks cover encrypted persistence, no client or capability provider calls while held, local expiry, count/byte limits, four-run concurrency, failed send-intent persistence, cancellation, late continuations, and restart without replay. Live relays exercise a lost search reply followed by an explicit retry with a different request binding. The pairwise integration exercises scoped consent, pending-message recovery, fetch, and acknowledgement. Desktop HTTP checks prove all three background mailbox paths stay quiet across lock/unlock, an in-flight automatic check stops without continuing to other mailboxes, results survive, and removing pilot configuration does not strand saved work. A browser check with the real cached model and two local relays verified Save search, reload/re-authenticate, Run, Save mailbox check, persistence through lock/unlock, and explicit completion with automatic checks still paused. It also verified the corrected Lock screen.

Checkpoint validation passed TypeScript/frontend compilation and 90 test files / 471 tests. The regression run exposed an existing churn-harness race: after journal loss, waiting for two active publications did not guarantee that the particular mailbox's publication had returned, because three records needed repair. The harness now waits for all three and reports unexpected mailbox responses explicitly; relay behavior was not changed for that fix. These local checks do not establish quiet-period anonymity. Desktop wallet integration and broader observer measurements remain open.

## Research basis

- The authors' [Anonymity Trilemma](https://freedom.cs.purdue.edu/projects/trilemma.html) analysis formalizes tradeoffs between latency, overhead, traffic patterns, and strong anonymity under specified adversaries. It supports rejecting an unconditional cheap-and-fast quiet-period guarantee; it is not a proof about this implementation.
- [Loopix](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity17/technical-sessions/presentation/piotrowska) combines cover traffic and randomized mixing in a complete architecture. Its guarantees cannot be transferred to our two-hop queues by adding dummy requests.
- [Tor's connection-padding specification](https://spec.torproject.org/padding-spec/connection-level-padding.html) targets connection metadata and supports resource-aware padding behavior. Its mechanism and overhead are not equivalent to Resonance's complete padded operation/reply exchanges.

Revisit this decision when a measured alternative improves protection against the specified observer within explicit owner budgets. Changing the delivery deadline, requiring continuous traffic, or claiming quiet-period anonymity requires new evidence; another low score for the simple rank attack is insufficient.
