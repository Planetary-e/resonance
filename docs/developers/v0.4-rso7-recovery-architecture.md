## 3. Why this is structurally different from RSO5/RSO6

### 3.1 Historical evidence

RSO5 is a bounded research prototype, not production acceptance evidence. Its complete measured work is **677,973,227 B**, against the original cumulative cap of 268,435,456 B; 28 operations exceed the original per-operation comparison, and maximum local recovery is **17,741,154 B**. The retained evidence is `docs/evals/v04-rso5-cost-analysis-2026-10-26.md` and `delivery/results/rso5-operation-work.json`.

The reconstruction and replication causes are visible in existing source:

- `validateProposal()` calls `expected()`, which calls `nextState()` (`packages/relay/src/sustainable-rso5-runtime.ts:196`).
- `prepare()` additionally evaluates its executed speculative journal (`:237`), and `execute()` validates and evaluates again (`:258`).
- Prepare constructs and encodes prepared, commit, executed, and finished journals for preflight (`:245`).
- Every journal write publishes a checkpoint and then writes the complete encoded journal (`:153`).
- The protected arena seals, base64-encodes, signs, locally writes, and five-replica replicates complete state (`packages/relay/src/sustainable-rso5-protected-arena.ts:322`).
- Journal decoding retrieves current policy, every activation, and any transition (`packages/relay/src/sustainable-recovery-codec.ts:192`).
- Checkpoint publication walks the complete committed checkpoint chain (`sustainable-rso5-protected-arena.ts:150`).

RSO6 already proposed single `nextState()` evaluation, snapshots, and deltas; those names alone therefore do not distinguish RSO7. RSO6 was rejected with first cold reserve **4,789,307 B**, **70,715 B** over `W`, and an incomplete cumulative lower bound of **311,198,374 B**. See `docs/developers/v0.4-rso6-recovery-architecture.md`. This document does not tune RSO5/RSO6 or reset their failures.

### 3.2 RSO7 protocol break

RSO7 removes the per-agreement-phase journal-generation model rather than caching or re-encoding it:

| RSO5/RSO6 obligation | RSO7 replacement |
| --- | --- |
| Proposal/prepare/commit/execute/finish state images | One pending transition, one committed suffix entry, and stable cuts only when due |
| Repeated `nextState()` in validation and execution | One independent original-reducer evaluation per voting member before that member's first vote |
| Votes/certificates containing or accompanying repeated journal state | Compact votes over a transition digest; bounded certificates containing signer/signature pairs |
| Reducer replay after crash | Application of certified semantic mutations |
| Snapshot plus speculative future journal encodings | Exact size-only preflight over actual snapshot, suffix, evidence, delegation, index, head, and failed-extent records |
| Checkpoint signer attesting only availability/order | Four transition voters attesting that they independently validated the exact business transition |
| Per-proof remote delegation discovery during recovery | One authenticated delegation restoration followed by scope-key reuse |

This is a protocol change, not a cache, threshold change, or representation tweak.

**Independent evaluation is mandatory.** One global leader evaluation followed by followers blindly signing its result is unsafe and is explicitly rejected. Each voting member, including the leader, independently evaluates the original transition at most once, before its PREPARE vote. Prepare, commit, execute, finish, retry, view change, and recovery never rerun the reducer. The intended bound is therefore one original-reducer evaluation per voting member per committed command, not one evaluation across the cohort. A leader-trusting variant would require a validity-proof system that does not exist in the inspected codebase.

## 4. Canonical state, evidence, and binary records

### 4.1 Canonical semantic state

A stable cut contains complete canonical semantic state, not original proof plaintext and not the agreement journal. It includes:

- Original genesis and context identity.
- Current configuration ID, global sequence, view, current policy head, observer head, and activation-history root.
- Complete authenticated configuration ancestry.
- Complete selected evidence root and exact frame-reference set.
- Separate vote/certificate indexes, including every competing binding.
- Every original accepted row: owner, issuer, spend, action, and binding.
- Every permanent denial.
- Current reconfiguration/terminal state and old-cohort fence.
- Current delegation and scope-key-version root.
- Prepared lock, committed floor, previous stable cut, and suffix head in the associated consensus cut.
- Authenticated incremental-index root.

No accepted row is collapsed into a “latest winner.” No denial, conflicting vote, alternative certificate, or split-inducing acceptance is discarded. `witnessConstraint()` must continue to distinguish `fresh`, `bound`, `split`, `conflict`, and `denied` from complete relevant evidence, as in `packages/relay/src/witness-handoff-state.ts:75`.

Original policy, activation, vote, certificate, request, and handoff bytes remain separate immutable evidence objects. A stable cut is not permission to prune them. Indexes are authenticated locators, never replacement evidence.

### 4.2 Proposed bounded binary records

These are new RSO7 design records, not existing RSO5/RSO6 constants:

| Record | Construction | Exact proposed bound |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Transition descriptor `T` | 28-byte version/view/sequence header plus 16 32-byte digests | **540 B** |
| Vote `V` | Magic/version/phase, transition digest, signer, Ed25519 signature | **141 B** |
| Four-vote certificate `Q` | Header, transition digest, count, four signer/signature pairs | **433 B** |
| Maximum semantic mutation `M` | 2,048-byte semantic row plus five references/digests | **2,208 B** |
| Suffix entry `Y` | 256-byte archive header + `T` + prepare QC + commit QC + length + `M` | **3,874 B** |
| Stable head `Z` | 256-byte archive header + transition/snapshot digest + four stable votes | **852 B** |
| Full index image | 256-byte archive header + 72 × 197-byte entries | **14,440 B** |
| Sealed state image | 65,536-byte state bound + 28-byte AEAD + 256-byte archive header | **65,820 B** |

The 16 descriptor digests bind, in order as protocol fields:

1. configuration;
2. previous transition;
3. previous stable cut;
4. previous state;
5. original command;
6. next state;
7. semantic mutation;
8. complete evidence;
9. index;
10. policy;
11. activation history;
12. observer state;
13. delegation;
14. terminal state/old-cohort fence;
15. funding/layout; and
16. genesis/context trust root.

All integers, lengths, counts, optional tags, and terminal lengths use strict canonical encodings. Same bytes with the same identity replay idempotently; the same identity with different bytes is a conflict. A valid prefix never makes a partial or torn record available.

The **2,208 B maximum mutation is a review obligation, not an established fact**. If any legitimate original command needs a larger semantic mutation, this forecast is invalid. Truncating a mutation or externalizing part of it without charging the resulting storage and work is forbidden.

## 5. Exact command, agreement, execution, and cut order

### Phase 0 — command admission and complete preflight

Before any externally visible or durable business effect, the leader:

1. Authenticates the exact original command and its signed domain.
2. Checks current configuration, policy freshness/cutoff, previous transition, old-cohort fence, competing-writer lock, and suffix capacity.
3. Computes exact physical sizes for evidence frames, pending transition, suffix entry, possible current+pending cut, indexes, delegation, heads, and complete failed extents.
4. Verifies prospective `C/L/D/P/F`, per-operation and cumulative work, aggregate bytes/entries/metadata, and funding for all required copies.
5. Refuses before effects if anything is unfunded, ambiguous, stale, conflicting, or unbounded.

The suffix bound is **four entries**. A fifth command refuses until a stable cut completes. This is a new RSO7 protocol bound, not a relaxation of RSO5's 32-slot model.

### Phase 1 — deterministic proposal and pending durability

The leader independently evaluates the original command once and constructs `T` and `M`. It proposes the original command together with the deterministic transition material and configuration continuity. Every voting member, including the leader, then:

1. Independently evaluates the same original command once against its own canonical state and complete relevant evidence; it does not trust the leader's result.
2. Requires byte-identical `T`, `M`, evidence root, policy root, and configuration ancestry.
3. Appends every new original evidence frame and fsyncs it.
4. Obtains four exact evidence acknowledgments from its five opaque storage replicas.
5. Appends the pending transition and mutation, fsyncs file data and metadata, and obtains four matching pending acknowledgments.
6. Only after that durable pending barrier emits a **PREPARE** vote on `digest(T)`.

An atomic file replacement barrier includes temporary-file fsync, atomic rename, and parent-directory fsync where required by the platform. An acknowledgment sent before a replica's own durable barrier is invalid. Directory metadata, allocation metadata, and all required index/head updates are included in the applicable barrier.

A crash after evaluation but before this barrier produces no vote. If the command later commits without that member, the member imports the certified mutation rather than reevaluating the command.

### Phase 2 — prepared certificate and durable lock

Four identical PREPARE votes from distinct currently authorized members form the prepared QC. Before emitting a COMMIT vote, each member must:

1. Verify all four signatures and the exact configuration, sequence, view, and transition digest.
2. Match the QC to its own durable pending transition.
3. Append the prepared QC and set its prepared lock.
4. Fsync locally and obtain four matching prepared-QC acknowledgments.

A member never signs COMMIT for a value lacking this durable prepared barrier. A prepared value is a lock, not an orphan and not yet a business effect.

### Phase 3 — commit certificate, suffix durability, then execute

Four identical COMMIT votes from distinct authorized members form the commit QC. Each member then, in this order:

1. Appends exactly one suffix entry `Y` containing `T`, both QCs, and the exact certified mutation `M`.
2. Advances its committed floor to the transition digest.
3. Fsyncs locally and obtains four exact suffix/floor acknowledgments.
4. Applies `M` to its in-memory semantic state. **This is execute; no reducer runs.**
5. Updates affected index entries and recomputes the affected index root.

A commit QC is not an external completion. No durable client acknowledgment, completion notification, old-cohort freeze, readiness export, or successor funding receipt is permitted yet.

### Phase 4 — stable vote and durable acknowledgment

After the suffix and executed state are durable, each member signs a compact STABLE vote on the transition and current storage/evidence/delegation roots.

If a full cut is due, the complete pending semantic snapshot and index must already have four durable copies **before** the STABLE vote; that vote then binds the snapshot digest as well as the transition digest.

Four STABLE votes form the stable certificate for the suffix entry. The stable head is locally installed, fsynced, and acknowledged by four replicas before any of the following:

- returning a durable client acknowledgment;
- issuing a completion-dependent notification;
- freezing an old cohort; or
- exporting readiness or successor funding receipts.

A crash after execute but before this stable barrier leaves an uncertain client outcome, not a lost acknowledged operation and not permission to emit a second outcome.

### Phase 5 — cut promotion and retirement

At an explicit cut or when the four-entry suffix is full:

1. Current and pending complete snapshots coexist and are both funded.
2. Pending state, index, evidence references, delegation root, and stable head all have four durable copies.
3. Four stable votes authorize promotion.
4. The new head is atomically installed and fsynced with the required directory barrier.
5. Only after promotion may covered suffix entries and the previous generation retire.

No mixed old/new generation is selectable. Retirement cannot remove original immutable evidence or delegations still required by authorization, audit, or private recovery.

## 6. Crash safety, orphans, pending values, and fences

### 6.1 Durable-copy matrix

The matrix concerns copies at the five opaque storage replicas. Any count below four, including two, is insufficient. “No business effect” does not erase work already expended.

| Stage | 0 copies | 1 or 3 copies | 4 copies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pending proposal | Previous committed state; no business effect | Pending only; no business effect | Eligible to form prepared QC; still no effect |
| Prepared QC | No new commit may be based on it; prior floor remains | Preserve as pending lock; no execute/acknowledgment | Commit votes permitted |
| Commit QC/suffix | No acknowledgment; prepared lock prevents conflicting replacement | Valid commit is a committed floor; catch up before service | Execute permitted; stable barrier still required |
| Pending full cut | Previous cut + suffix remain authoritative | Pending unavailable; previous generation retained | Stable promotion permitted |
| Stable head | Previous head remains | New head pending; no pruning | Stable state selectable and acknowledgment permitted |

If four commit votes were emitted but no commit-QC copy survives, recovery still cannot safely select a conflicting value: the previously required four prepared copies and highest-prepared lock force the same transition into the next view. Loss of all usable prepared/committed copies is loss of quorum and stops progress; it is not permission to accept an alternative.

### 6.2 Orphans, pending work, and retries

- Evidence not referenced by a committed transition is an orphan. It has no business effect and cannot satisfy an evidence root.
- Undeclared orphan evidence is rejected during preflight before append.
- Declared orphan evidence consumes `C`; all work and failed-attempt traffic already expended remain charged.
- Prepared values are not orphans; they are locks and must participate in view change.
- A committed but unacknowledged command has an uncertain client outcome. An exact retry resolves that outcome and never creates a second acceptance or notification.
- Partial/torn suffix, head, or snapshot records never become available from a valid prefix.

`F` remains **32,768 B**. Complete failed extents are retained only where they fit. If the complete failed extent cannot fit `F`, repair refuses before modifying the surviving generation. A large torn snapshot therefore causes refusal, not an invented successful recovery.

### 6.3 Competing writers and old-cohort fencing

- A local single-writer lease plus `(configuration, sequence, previousState)` compare-and-append prevents two local writers from committing different children of one state.
- Exact same-command replay returns the existing certified outcome.
- Same request identity with different bytes, parent, action, or binding is a conflict.
- A configuration change becomes effective only at its stable terminal cut with successor reservations and authenticated activation.
- After that cut, old-configuration proposals, votes, checkpoints, delegations, and writes are rejected regardless of view or sequence.
- The new cohort starts at the same global committed sequence; it never resets history.
- Old members retain required evidence and delegations until the successor has four complete copies and local installation. They cannot authorize new business after the fence.

Four-of-five quorum intersection prevents two conflicting four-signature certificates under at most one Byzantine signer. The protocol must nevertheless retain equivocation evidence and treat it as conflict rather than assuming signers are honest.

## 7. Recovery and configuration continuity

### 7.1 Cold recovery

A cold node must:

1. Obtain exact stable-head statuses from four replicas and group them by digest.
2. Verify the stable certificate against the configuration authorized **at that historical sequence**, never against a union of old, new, and historical members.
3. Verify continuous ancestry to original genesis through original policy and activation objects.
4. Fetch one exact state/index payload after four matching statuses.
5. Restore required delegations once, privately unwrap scope keys, and reuse them across proofs.
6. Recover all selected evidence using four exact statuses and one exact payload per object.
7. Apply at most four certified suffix mutations without invoking business reducers.
8. Recompute semantic, evidence, index, delegation, and configuration roots.
9. Refuse service until current fresh policy and every four-copy floor are restored.

A recent snapshot without the stable certificate and original authorization continuity is not trusted. Missing evidence, incompatible ancestry, stale policy, or an unavailable four-copy floor causes refusal.

### 7.2 Returning-node recovery

A returning node compares exact stable head, evidence root, delegation root, and configuration ancestry. It transfers only missing state, suffix entries, frames, and delegations, but still proves four-copy availability. It cannot serve while a higher local committed floor, prepared lock, missing proof, stale delegation, or stale policy remains unresolved.

### 7.3 New-view recovery

Four view-change records contain:

- current stable certificate;
- committed floor;
- highest prepared QC;
- configuration and previous-state identity; and
- exact state/evidence/delegation availability references.

The new leader selects the highest valid committed floor and preserves the highest prepared value. A local node with a higher committed floor catches up before installing the new view. Missing predecessor state, a skipped sequence, an omitted lock, or incompatible same-sequence cuts refuses the new view. The NEW_VIEW identity is durably installed before any proposal in that view.

### 7.4 Complete local-`C` loss

Local-`C` loss uses four-status/one-payload evidence recovery, one delegation restoration, one state/index import, and up to four suffix applications. It must not repeat full-list delegation discovery for every proof, unlike the current RSO5 recovery behavior at `packages/relay/src/sustainable-rso5-protected-arena.ts:171`.

If the remote four-copy floor is also unavailable, this becomes cold/quorum recovery rather than cheaper local repair. No service is permitted until authorization continuity, policy freshness, private scope-key restoration, and all storage floors are reestablished.

## 8. Role privacy, exact verifier reuse, and indexes

### 8.1 Role privacy

Retain `RSO5F001` and `RSO5D001` unchanged:

- Original plaintext digests and original business signatures remain unchanged.
- Per-proof DEKs and scope-key versions remain unchanged.
- HPKE recipient enrollment and exporter/recipient authorization remain unchanged.
- Replicas receive ciphertext, signed metadata, compact votes, and sealed semantic state only.
- No shared replica-plaintext state or cross-scope scope key is introduced.
- Removed recipients receive no new delegation; no retroactive erasure of plaintext they already obtained is claimed.

The original frame format and signature verification are at `packages/relay/src/sustainable-rso5-opaque.ts:58`.

### 8.2 Exact original verifier composition

Business evaluation must extract and reuse the existing transition body, not create a second implementation of business semantics. The composition includes:

- `verifyAdmissionWitnessVote`
- `verifyAdmissionSpendCertificate`
- `verifyAdmissionWitnessRequest`
- `verifyAdmissionPolicy`
- `parseWitnessHandoffProposal` / `parseWitnessHandoffActivation`
- `mergeWitnessEvidence`
- `witnessConstraint`

Each voting member invokes this composition once for its first prepared value. The resulting mutation is certified and reused by commit, execute, retry resolution, view change, and recovery.

The extracted composition must not accidentally verify the same certificate twice. If the existing reducer verifies once directly and again inside `witnessConstraint(..., originalCertificate)`, the extraction must pass already authenticated evidence in a semantically equivalent way and prove byte-identical outcomes differentially. Any discrepancy in acceptance, denial, conflict, split, rights, cutoff, retry, or uncertainty is a stop condition.

### 8.3 Index equivalence

Indexes are locators, never alternative evidence:

- `(issuer, spend, set)` maps to **all** vote/certificate references and all bindings.
- Configuration/activation IDs map to exact original objects.
- Every index entry carries proof ID, frame digest, binding digest, kind, and flags.
- Every transition recomputes affected entries and the index root.
- A missing index entry causes refusal or authenticated full lookup, never a default `fresh` result.
- Cold/import recovery checks index entries against the canonical selected reference set.
- Differential gates compare incremental results with full original-evidence reduction for every acceptance, denial, conflict, split, retry, and restart.

### 9.6 Nominal binary network model and unresolved resources

The nominal model charges 518,280 B per non-cut command and an **additional** 1,316,400 B full-cut replication charge every fourth command. Its stated amortization is therefore:

```text
518,280 + 1,316,400/4 = 847,380 B per completed command
```

The word “additional” is necessary for consistency with the source total. This forecast excludes TLS framing, discovery, retransmission, failed-attempt variation, and scheduled observer/privacy traffic. It therefore does not close K7's 1 MiB completed-operation network limit.

K7, 3 s/30 s deadlines, memory, idle CPU, and energy remain unknown. No paper benchmark supplies these values.

## 10. Executable fault-schedule plan and controls

This is a predeclared plan for a possible future, separately authorized implementation review. **No schedule was run for this document.** A real future harness must freeze the candidate and schedule identities, inject actual process/storage death at each barrier, enumerate 0/1/3/4 durable copies (and treat two as insufficient), preserve failed work, and check externally visible outcomes as well as final state.

### 10.1 Required schedules

| Schedule | Injection / adversarial action | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FS-01 admission | Die before and during each preflight calculation; introduce unfunded `C/L/D/P/F`, aggregate, or work | Refusal before business effect; no undeclared append; attempted work charged |
| FS-02 evidence barrier | Die after local evidence append and at 0/1/3/4 exact acknowledgments | No PREPARE before four; orphan evidence has no business effect; complete evidence remains recoverable at four |
| FS-03 pending barrier | Die after reducer evaluation, pending append, rename, and directory fsync at each copy count | No vote without durable pending; later commit imports mutation instead of rerunning reducer |
| FS-04 prepared lock | Die before/after prepared-QC append and lock at 0/1/3/4 copies | No COMMIT without four; surviving lock fences conflicts; no execute or acknowledgment |
| FS-05 lost commit QC | Emit four COMMIT votes but retain zero commit-QC copies while prepared copies/locks survive | Next view carries the same highest-prepared value; no conflicting acceptance |
| FS-06 suffix/floor | Die during suffix append, floor update, and four-replica acknowledgment | Valid suffix is caught up before service; partial/torn entry is unavailable; no external completion |
| FS-07 execute boundary | Die after durable suffix, during in-memory `M` application, and during index update | Restart applies certified mutation without reducer; canonical state/index roots agree; stable acknowledgment still withheld |
| FS-08 stable barrier | Die before/after STABLE vote, stable-head install, and each acknowledgment count | No client acknowledgment, notification, freeze, readiness receipt, or pruning before four durable stable copies |
| FS-09 cut promotion | Die with current+pending generations during snapshot, index, head rename, directory fsync, and retirement | Select either complete old generation+suffix or complete promoted generation, never a mixed generation |
| FS-10 view change | Omit highest lock, skip sequence, present incompatible same-sequence cuts, or die before NEW_VIEW durability | Refuse new view/proposal; highest valid committed floor and prepared value survive |
| FS-11 equivocation | One Byzantine member signs competing values or conflicting votes | No conflicting four-signature certificate; equivocation retained as conflict; honest state does not split |
| FS-12 old cohort | Submit old proposals/votes/delegations/writes after terminal stable cut and crash during successor installation | Old cohort cannot authorize new business; evidence/delegations retained until successor is installed with four copies |
| FS-13 cold recovery | Supply stale snapshot, wrong historical signers, union-of-cohorts certificate, broken genesis ancestry, missing proof, stale policy | Refuse service; valid historical authorization plus four-copy floors alone can recover |
| FS-14 returning recovery | Return with higher local floor, prepared lock, missing frames/delegations, stale roots | Catch up and restore four-copy availability before service; no reducer replay |
| FS-15 local-`C` loss | Lose all local private evidence with remote four-copy floor present, then with quorum absent | One delegation restoration and bounded transfer when available; otherwise cold/quorum recovery or refusal |
| FS-16 failed extents | Tear maximum suffix/snapshot/head; vary complete failed extent around `F=32,768` | Retain exact complete failed extent if funded; otherwise refuse before modifying surviving generation |
| FS-17 retry/orphan | Retry exact command after uncertain commit; alter bytes/parent/action/binding under same identity | Exact retry resolves one outcome without duplicate notice; altered identity is conflict; orphan work remains charged |
| FS-18 competing writers | Race two local children of one `(configuration, sequence, previousState)` | At most one compare-and-append child commits; no conflicting acceptance |
| FS-19 privacy/authorization | Removed recipient requests new delegation; replica attempts plaintext access; cross-scope key reuse attempted | No new delegation/plaintext/cross-scope key; original recipient authorization remains authoritative |
| FS-20 resource exhaustion | Exhaust reservation bytes/entries/metadata, suffix capacity, failed-extent space, and work headroom | Bounded refusal before effects; no cap relaxation, truncation, or uncharged externalization |

Schedules must also cover lost replies, partitions, stale restores, corrupt input, predecessor-offline cold recovery, same-key witness replacement, policy cutoff during disconnection, >64-configuration ancestry, and repeated compaction. A thousand repetitions of one happy path do not satisfy K2.

### 10.2 Positive controls

A future qualifying suite must include, at minimum:

- A no-fault four-copy command proving exact durable order and one reducer invocation per voting member.
- Differential original-reducer results for acceptance, permanent denial, conflict, split, retry, restart, policy cutoff, and same-key replacement.
- Incremental-index versus full-original-evidence equivalence for every result class.
- Crash recovery after durable commit and after stable acknowledgment with zero lost acknowledged operations and zero duplicate notifications.
- Cold, returning, new-view, and local-`C` recovery using four statuses/one payload and at most four mutation applications.
- Current+pending cut promotion followed by safe retirement and complete authorization continuity.
- Exact same-command retry returning the original certified outcome.
- Funding success only after complete prospective physical/work closure, including temporary and failed extents.

These are required controls, not existing RSO7 results.

### 10.3 Negative controls

The suite must reject each of the following:

- A leader-produced `T/M` signed by followers without independent evaluation.
- PREPARE before durable pending, COMMIT before durable prepared QC, execute before durable suffix, or acknowledgment/pruning before durable stable head.
- Three-signature certificates, cross-configuration signatures, wrong historical cohorts, or a stock two-reply client receipt substituted for four durable business confirmations.
- Conflicting same-identity retry, missing index treated as `fresh`, omitted competing binding, or discarded permanent denial.
- Uncertified recent snapshots, rolled-back stable heads, stale policy/delegation, mixed cut generations, and post-fence old-cohort writes.
- Partial records accepted from valid prefixes or failed extents silently discarded when complete retention does not fit `F`.
- A mutation larger than 2,208 B being truncated or externalized without charged storage/work.
- Replica plaintext, new delegation to removed recipients, cross-scope keys, or replacement of original signatures with engine keys.
- Any zero-cost failure, retry, orphan, observer, network, metadata, or recovery phase.

A reproducible safety violation blocks adoption regardless of averages or forecast headroom.

## 11. Alternatives comparison and established-protocol context

### 11.1 Why full BFT-SMaRt adoption is rejected for this pass

Candidate B is rejected as an integration/economic choice under DEC-014, not because BFT-SMaRt is universally unsafe and not because a measured RSO7-versus-BFT-SMaRt experiment was run.

| Dimension | RSO7 Candidate A | Full BFT-SMaRt Candidate B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original reducer | Retains exact TypeScript implementation | Non-sidecar adoption requires a byte-exact Java port and differential proof |
| Agreement | New custom protocol proof remains necessary | Established ordering/view-change implementation |
| Execution | One independent deterministic evaluation per voting member, then mutation reuse | Normal state-machine replication executes at each active replica |
| Durability/recovery | Certified cuts directly target the measured reconstruction problem | Stock durability/state transfer still needs replacement or wrapping for funding, privacy, original authority, and crash order |
| Four-copy availability | Explicit in every barrier | Votes and state hashes do not provide it; remains custom |
| Configuration continuity | Must be proven | Must still be proven, plus view-manager/TTP, numeric-ID, key, and `currentView` adaptation |
| Operational footprint | Existing Node runtime | Java/JVM/Gradle/Netty/Bouncy Castle plus retained Node UI/client surfaces |
| Resource case | Bottom-up forecast exists but is unmeasured | No credible bottom-up forecast shows an advantage in `W`, cumulative work, storage, JVM/relay memory, or 3 s/30 s deadlines |

The decisive points are:

1. **Java migration.** A genuinely non-sidecar engine requires the live deterministic business core and storage/funding boundaries in the JVM. Keeping the live TypeScript reducer as a sidecar would recreate the already rejected two-runtime shape. Original signed-byte construction, JavaScript field/number behavior, Ed25519, AES-GCM/HKDF, HPKE, NaCl boxes, and Argon2id behavior require explicit preservation or differential proof.
2. **Custom durability and recovery remain.** The recommended basic recoverable path uses an in-memory request log plus snapshots. `DiskStateLog` is not transactionally aligned with funded current/pending replacement, directory fsync, complete failed extents, and four-copy evidence. Collaborative durability opens a separate Java-deserialization socket and has the standard certified-decision condition commented out. A custom `Recoverable` and authenticated private recovery plane remain necessary.
3. **Receipts and continuity remain custom.** Five members with `f=1` yield four normal-path accepts because decision uses `countAccept(value) > quorumBFT`, where the stored quorum is three. The stock ordered client uses only `f+1=2` matching replies, so Resonance still needs a four-response original-signature receipt. Engine views do not distribute original-genesis policy, activation, recipient, or delegation authority.
4. **No credible resource advantage.** Source inspection cannot bound serialization, checkpoint/suffix peaks, retry rounds, JVM/Netty/GC memory, Java reducer copies, five-runtime traffic, or impaired-network timing. The RSO6 private-recovery comparison already had only 264,936 B below `W`; that is a screening constraint, not proof Candidate B must overflow and not a license to tune RSO6.

A future reconsideration would first need a custom-recoverer design and schedules for death around execution/log/checkpoint replacement, two-versus-four replies, Byzantine state/log/view senders, predecessor-offline recovery, 0/1/3/4 copies, failed extents, stale-view rollback, and >64 configurations. That is outside this comparison pass.

### 11.3 PBFT and state-transfer references are context, not RSO7 proof

The established PBFT protocol is described by Castro and Liskov, “Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance and Proactive Recovery” (OSDI 2002), and the original PBFT paper, “Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance” (OSDI 1999): <http://pmg.csail.mit.edu/papers/osdi99.pdf> and <http://pmg.csail.mit.edu/papers/osdi02.pdf>. These establish the general quorum, view-change, checkpoint, and recovery context; they do not establish this design's exact durable-copy barriers, private evidence semantics, funding rules, or binary bounds.

BFT-SMaRt's `ApplicationState`, `DefaultRecoverable`, and `StandardStateManager` primary sources above document an established Byzantine-aware state-transfer shape: authenticated/checkpointed state plus subsequent batches, hash/proof checks, and suffix handling. They are useful review references for RSO7's stable cuts and bounded suffixes. **Neither PBFT nor BFT-SMaRt state transfer is presented as proof of RSO7 correctness.** RSO7 still needs its own reducer-equivalence argument, quorum/view-change proof, durable-barrier schedules, original-authority continuity, privacy analysis, and measured resource closure.

## 12. Review obligations, falsifiers, and stop rules

The one independent DEC-014 review must address at least:

1. Exact original-reducer equivalence, including no duplicate certificate verification and byte-identical semantic outcomes.
2. Independent per-member evaluation, quorum intersection, prepared-lock/view-change safety, and resistance to one Byzantine member.
3. Every durable barrier, actual process-death schedule, atomic replacement metadata, and 0/1/3/4-copy behavior.
4. Complete canonical state, evidence retention, old-cohort fencing, configuration ancestry, and cold/recovery policy freshness.
5. The 2,208 B mutation bound, strict binary schemas, suffix bound, index equivalence, and all physical partitions.
6. Primitive-by-primitive reconstruction of operation totals not expanded in the Candidate A source.
7. Complete cumulative inventory, including observer/policy, pending, orphan, retry, failed-extent, network, and failure work.
8. Aggregate bytes/entries/metadata, temporary replacement peaks, and complete prospective funding.
9. 3 s/30 s deadlines, K7 network/memory/CPU/energy, and the separate 100-transition/>64-configuration qualification path.
10. Role privacy, original signatures, delegation restoration, removed recipients, and absence of replica plaintext.

Stop RSO7 and request a concrete owner decision on the first applicable condition:

- Any reproducible safety failure or structural counterexample, including conflicting acceptance, lost acknowledgment, duplicate notification, authorization rollback, stale-policy service, privacy breach, or evidence loss.
- Any leader-trusting execution, reducer mismatch, unsafe cut/fault schedule, invalid quorum/certificate, or omitted highest-prepared lock.
- Any legitimate mutation exceeding 2,208 B without a newly reviewed and fully charged design.
- Physical overflow of `C/L/P/D/F`, aggregate bytes/entries/metadata, or failure to retain a complete required failed extent.
- Per-operation or cumulative work above the original caps. In particular, omitted maximum-local-recovery work greater than **105,145 B** invalidates the present per-operation forecast.
- Incomplete original-reducer equivalence, four-copy availability, original-genesis continuity, or complete prospective funding.
- Progress requiring an unavailable external measurement or a changed trust/resource/deadline assumption. Record the precise gap rather than inferring success.
- The persistent project stop policy is also unchanged: two successive substantive alternatives without distinguishable meaningful improvement, or three substantive alternatives/five accumulated active workdays on the blocker, trigger an owner decision; historical alternatives and unknown effort are not reset.

A review finding cannot be answered by tuning RSO3–RSO6/CometBFT constants, rerunning stopped campaigns, enlarging a fixture, invoking DEC-012, or relabeling forecast fit as acceptance. The available decisions after review are a separately authorized bounded continuation, redesign, suspension for a named dependency, or stopping the approach. None closes R1/R2 or K1–K8 by itself.
