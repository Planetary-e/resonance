# v0.4 completion work

The active objective is to complete every v0.4 roadmap item and its necessary
dependencies. The existing branch is `feature/v0.4-private-transport`, based on
the corrected v0.3 work. Implementation, tests, documentation and the draft PR
advance together. This work does not authorize a merge, installer or release.

## Ordered implementation work

| Privacy and resources | Quantified curious/colluding-relay, multi-domain Sybil and timing observers; total traffic, concurrent/near-capacity costs and energy | Reproducible traces, stated privacy targets, measured costs and honest reporting of targets not met |
| Fingerprint epochs | Live group/epoch scoping, epoch-boundary migration and real-text quality/privacy evaluation | Matching recall/precision, cross-scope correlation and compatibility tests with the actual embedding path |
| Completion scenario | Private publish/search/mailbox with no account or credential link across two valid operations | Full application integration and a reviewed release gate covering all assumptions above |

Each implementation checkpoint must leave evidence and an accurate roadmap.
An implementation test is not substituted for an external measurement, a bounded
model is not a general proof, and a design decision alone does not finish its
implementation requirement. Derived work belongs under the area that needs it;
discovering a limitation must not silently narrow the original completion target.

## External validation requirements

The roadmap also requires independently operated volunteer overlap, real
Internet/TLS paths, device power/energy and independent-network reachability
measurements. The available single computer can run real local processes,
cryptography, TLS and fault injection, plus explicitly labeled network simulation.
Those results cannot certify independent operators or real volunteer schedules.
Build reproducible pilot tooling and finish all independent implementation work
before treating missing external access or observations as a blocker.

Keep new admission/recovery behavior opt-in until its durability, membership and
freshness gates are actually satisfied. Do not clear old ledgers, reset histories,
discard denial markers or waive a privacy target merely to mark a checkbox done.

### Preserved integration constraints

Agreement must persist its selected proposal before choosing a download cache.
A carried higher-view proposal can differ from the owner's preferred proposal;
reusing a cached history must verify the actual selected root/head and all frozen
anchors. Interrupted download must retain progress without silently restarting
agreement or changing its value. Historical data can supply immutable ciphertext,
but every new agreement phase still needs fresh authenticated requests and its
own durable proof. Keep the existing operation deadlines.

Live repair needs complete recoverable ciphertext for the writer's current
generation, not just its latest receipt. Protected admission must durably retain
the exact pending update before transmission and require four matching durable
receipts before accepting it locally. Recovery must preserve spends, permanent
denials and the authenticated policy together; a complete old cache alone cannot
replace those live-state/freshness requirements.

### Durable owner retention and admission boundary

The [retained writer](v0.4-history-writer-retention.md) now retains immutable
ciphertext before transmission, with an encrypted ordered index and exact writer
anchors. Reopen validates all indexed data and only permits the pending writer's
known next orphan. Retry reuses the original ciphertext. The protected admission
adapter now preserves this ordering and uses the retained writer API.

Avoid re-reading and re-verifying the entire retained journal for every token.
The generic iterable `retained()` repair source does a bounded full traversal per
attempt; it is suitable for explicit catch-up but has cumulative quadratic work
over a growing history. The retained writer now authenticates its chain on open,
validates appends, exposes a registered local digest index and revalidates payloads
read during repair. Its index is still atomically rewritten per append. Measure
the resulting near-capacity CPU, memory and I/O behavior rather than claiming a
constant total cost.

The live protected adapter serializes updates, rejects competing work without an
unbounded queue, and persists recoverable pending intent before network I/O. Four
durable receipts precede success. Lost replies and cutoffs during replication
preserve committed evidence and its original request binding. Exact attributed
replay allocates no new block; legacy attribution is a protected mutation.

The baseline/reducer covers authenticated policy, known/unattributed spends and
permanent denial markers. Explicit same-file promotion captures an intact
existing ledger under its lock. Empty/missing files cannot initialize an old
history. Retirement, legacy recovery and policy changes obey the same protected
boundary. Legacy recovery replicates the honest owner's verified attribution,
not raw bearer evidence or an independently verifiable token proof.

`AdmissionSpendLedger.write()` now permits an asynchronous write, awaited by the
verifier, with final cutoff/close checks. The separate `beforeSpend` witness hook
continues to authorize the spend; it is not the local durability boundary.

The v3/v4 format fence rejects local-only readers, including the old offline
maintenance commands. The complete protected policy and spend image move together;
the separate policy file may advance before startup finishes replication, but no
service is returned in that state. Configured relay startup now reopens prepared
protected history, accounts its sockets and awaits fresh readiness before listening.
Preparation, enrollment and serve commands are now available. Restore and
maintenance commands remain to be wired. Full-ancestry semantic replay and reviewed new-directory successor
activation are implemented as APIs. An ordinary reopen now also needs fresh
exact-head acknowledgements; a historical certificate can finish interrupted local
apply but cannot establish readiness. Safe compaction and complete policy/witness
recovery remain. The next integration work is explicit operator restore and
maintenance, followed by history transport over authenticated reverse relay links
and desktop service lifecycle integration, without automatic membership selection
or missing-history initialization.

### Operator recovery requirements

Initial preparation/enrollment is now executable, but it must not be presented as
lost-owner recovery. The current restoration APIs receive a selected proposal and
certified continuation from their caller. A real lost-owner command must work from
retained keys, context and independently pinned genesis even when its old local
generation-selection files are gone. The checkpoint exchange currently returns
heads/blocks, not an authenticated activated-successor lookup. Add bounded,
owner-authenticated discovery of certified continuation ancestry before composing
the operator recovery workflow. A peer's claimed generation number or local
directory name cannot choose the next root.

The end-to-end acceptance case must erase the owner's working directory after at
least two generation changes, retain only its independently backed-up keys/context/
genesis and current authenticated policy, then recover all accepted spends and
permanent denials from the volunteers. Preserve the actual carried agreement
selection, explicit irreversible freeze, resumable download/provisioning, reviewed
new-directory activation and fresh four-member readiness. Historical data or a
serialized review alone must not reopen admission. Test stale and unavailable
sources, interrupted commands and refusal to reconstruct from partial evidence.

The discovered 64 KiB delta versus 512 KiB policy mismatch is addressed by a full
policy checkpoint transition. It preserves the complete spend/fence state,
authenticates the signed successor and remains predecessor-linked. It does not
permit general snapshot replacement or compaction. Measure near-capacity archive
and live-image rewrite costs as part of the remaining resource work.
