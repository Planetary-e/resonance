# Protected admission history

`openProtectedAdmissionSpendHistory` and the optional `historyProtection` setting
on `createConfiguredAdmissionVerifier` connect the retained history writer to the
real blind-token admission boundary. A new spend cannot produce `accepted` until
four pinned replicas have durably acknowledged the exact encrypted history block.
An unavailable quorum produces `admission_unavailable` at the relay and the
publication is not stored. This is an opt-in generation-zero integration, not a
declaration that v0.4 admission or recovery is ready to enable by default.

## Commit ordering

1. Verify the token, request binding, current signed policy, permanent key fences
   and any required witness authorization.
2. Serialize the ledger mutation. A competing mutation is rejected, with no
   unbounded waiting queue or replacement of unresolved intent.
3. Atomically flush the exact encrypted pending block in the spend file.
4. The [retained writer](v0.4-history-writer-retention.md) persists its intent,
   payload and index, repairs compatible missing suffixes, obtains four fresh
   target-write receipts and flushes the certificate.
5. Atomically apply the mutation to the live spend image and clear pending state.
6. Recheck close, policy/key cutoff and permanent retirement before returning an
   admission success.

The original `beforeSpend` witness hook is insufficient for this ordering: it
precedes the local ledger write. `AdmissionSpendLedger.write` now permits an
asynchronous durable write, which the verifier awaits. The synchronous local and
legacy ledgers retain their existing storage behavior.

A write may become durable even when its reply is lost, the process dies, or the
new-spend cutoff passes during replication. Such a write stays consumed for its
original binding. It is never refunded or assigned to another request. An exact
retry of an attributed committed spend creates no new history block; legacy
attribution is itself a protected mutation. Existing witness policy still applies
to retries. Replicating this owner's ciphertext does not replace cross-relay
witness authorization or establish independent operators.

## Baseline and format fence

The protected v3 state replaces `admission-spends-v2.jsonl` atomically under its
existing lock and encryption domain. Local-only readers reject the new format.
Removing the protection configuration therefore cannot silently resume ordinary
local-only admission. The old offline maintenance reader also refuses it.

The encrypted baseline contains the complete authenticated signed policy,
attributed and unattributed spends, and permanent issuer denial markers. Its
policy digest must match the independently pinned history context. Promotion
requires `promoteLocalHistory: true` and an intact old ledger; opening an existing
missing, empty, corrupt or incompatible file fails. A raw legacy row remains
unattributed until real cryptographic evidence identifies it.

For a genuinely new ledger, initialization is explicit. Opening/enrollment sends
no network traffic. The caller obtains `enrollment()` and enrolls that exact root
on the five pinned replica services before invoking `ready()`. Writer and archive
creation have separate explicit flags for interrupted pristine setup; they cannot
recreate missing committed history. The configured verifier awaits `ready()`
before it returns an admission service. A separately prepared spend ledger can
use `initializeSpendHistory: false` when the policy/witness files are first created.

The production environment-variable entry point and an operator-facing enrollment
command are not wired yet. Do not invent an automatic empty baseline, silently
choose volunteers, or bypass required independent member pins to make startup
succeed. The exported API currently supports application-controlled enrollment.

## All mutations share the boundary

Owner-authenticated encrypted records carry:

- New spends and exact legacy attribution, with no bearer token.
- Signed policy successors, preserving old keys/cutoffs and permanent fences.
- Reviewed retirement of inactive keys after their final retry cutoff, with
  removal of only their attributed rows and an irreversible denial marker.
- Reviewed legacy recovery after local verification of real bearer evidence.
  The replicated record contains the proven spend-to-key attribution and fence,
  not the bearer proof. This is an authenticated statement by the honest owner;
  it is not a transferable proof that independently re-verifies that token.

The reducer validates exact fields, bounded bytes/entries, known issuer keys,
binding conflicts, policy transitions, cutoffs and denial markers. A recorded
`at` value is the owner's historical event time, not an independent trusted clock
or evidence of present freshness. Snapshot policy signatures are authenticated
even when their validity interval is historical.

The protected copy of policy and spend image moves together. The separate policy
installation file can advance before network replication; startup then refuses
service until the protected policy transition completes. Loss recovery must
eventually restore both consistently; this integration does not supply that
operator workflow.

## Restart and bounds

Reopen verifies the pinned context, baseline, signed image policy, pending
successor and retained writer/archive. Committed and pending heads must agree
across files. A writer certificate for the exact pending block can finish an
interrupted local apply without another network round. That historical certificate
does not authorize a different write. New writes still require fresh receipts;
a frozen generation rejects them.

`ready()` explicitly completes durable pending intent, including a denied spend
whose caller never received success. It does not replay the publication or send
its application acknowledgement. Further admission remains unavailable while
unresolved pending work exists. Lost replies reuse original ciphertext; a
different pending request is rejected.

Current limits remain 10,000 live spend rows, 8 MiB checkpoint plaintext, 64 KiB
delta plaintext, a 32 MiB encrypted live ledger, and the writer's bounded
10,001-block / 64 MiB ciphertext archive. Policy/retirement records also consume
history slots. Full history stops new work; nothing silently discards denial
evidence. The live image and archive index are atomically rewritten, so this does
not establish constant per-spend CPU/I/O or a total device storage bound.

The initial policy-mutation codec also uses the 64 KiB delta limit. V2 signed
policies may reach 512 KiB, so a sufficiently large valid successor currently
refuses protection instead of becoming a local-only policy update. Supporting
those policies needs a bounded full-checkpoint transition that verifies unchanged
spends/fences and the signed successor; this derived compatibility work is open.

## Evidence and remaining work

Tests use real policy signatures, blind tokens, durable files and five local
WebSocket replica services. They verify the actual relay publication response,
three-copy refusal, restart and exact retry, conflicting reuse, policy/retirement
and legacy recovery, frozen generations, pending concurrency, cutoff during
replication, file/directory flush failures and five SIGKILL boundaries. Ciphertext
reduction reconstructs the complete generation-zero policy/spend/fence image.

TypeScript and the complete local suite passed **148 files / 919 tests** in
**511.00 seconds** with one worker. All **352 TypeScript source hashes** remained
unchanged during that run. The protected-history file has 20 cases on macOS/Linux
(the directory-flush failure cases do not apply to Windows).

These are local protocol tests, not independent-volunteer Internet availability
or energy measurements. A coherent stale backup of every owner file still needs
the frozen agreement/ancestry protocol; local decryption alone cannot certify
freshness. Next work is operator integration, authenticated full-ancestry semantic
replay, reviewed atomic activation of a certified successor generation, safe
compaction and complete policy/spend/witness recovery. Admission stays opt-in.
