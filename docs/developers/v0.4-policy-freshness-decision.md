# Policy freshness: decision and first protocol boundary

## Current behavior and gap

A signed admission policy proves authority and content. Local revision retention
rejects rollback on that device; expiry bounds its use under the local clock.
None proves that another device has received a newer policy or that a partitioned
client has learned an emergency update. The existing policy store therefore does
not yet satisfy authenticated distributed freshness. This document preserves
that requirement rather than relabeling the existing expiry check as completion.

## Reference points

[TUF 1.0.36](https://theupdateframework.github.io/specification/v1.0.36/) separates
trusted root updates from fresh metadata: root changes advance sequentially and
need the prior and successor signature thresholds. Expiry bounds freeze attacks
under a trusted clock; it cannot force a hostile network to deliver an update.
We use these principles for the planned authority transition; this implementation
is not a TUF client and does not claim TUF compliance.

[Certificate Transparency](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) supplies
signed statements and consistency evidence. Comparing independently observed
views is still necessary to expose equivocation. A locally valid signed policy
alone cannot establish one globally shared view. The finite quorum analysis
below is our protocol analysis, not a claim made by either reference.

## Selected freshness contract

Keep policy authorization offline-capable. A separately pinned observer context
binds the community authority, initial policy revision/digest, and five volunteer
infrastructure keys/endpoints. Count four distinct current observations of the
same revision and digest. Each response signs a new random challenge, context
and a bounded request lifetime. No personal account, permit or credential appears
in this exchange. A caller must also authenticate the policy matching that digest
check its validity/cutoffs again before use, and enforce a monotonic operation
deadline. A signed observation cannot extend a policy expiry.

This can establish a head observed during that request. It cannot establish that
no later update exists, promise instantaneous revocation, or invalidate work
already accepted before an update. A new policy becomes globally effective only
after its activation is durably accepted by a quorum. One volunteer seeing a new
signature is insufficient. For overlapping activation/observation operations,
the guarantee must be stated relative to activation and request intervals.

The five keys represent a manually pinned trust set, not proof of five independent
operators. The selected fault assumption is at most one Byzantine observer,
with honest observers retaining monotone state and never approving two heads at
one revision. Both update activation and subsequent observation require four of
five. These quorums share at least three members, at least two honest under that
assumption. An honest retained update prevents an old-head observation quorum.

The finite model checks all 216 combinations of no/one faulty member, activation
quorums and observation quorums, plus conflicting same-revision approvals. It
retains counterexamples for three/three quorums, a locally seen unactivated update,
two honest observers losing their installed head, and replaying a prior observation.
Only six of the 32 reachability subsets contain four members; even those are not
sufficient if a reachable faulty member withholds a needed response. Independence,
schedules and latency require measurements, not subset counting.

## Implemented boundary

`admission-policy-freshness.ts` defines bounded observer contexts, requests, signed
votes and four/five-vote certificates. It binds the authority, genesis, member
keys/endpoints, exact challenge, time interval and expected revision/digest.
It rejects repeated signers, mixed heads, unknown signers, tampered signatures,
wrong contexts, expired/future requests, extra fields and invalid collections.
The 8 KiB certificate bound is independent of the full policy's larger byte limit.
The default request lifetime is three seconds, with a 30-second absolute maximum;
this is a protocol bound, not a measured Internet latency target.

Twenty-seven real-cryptography/finite-model tests pass in 526 ms. This is an
experimental protocol primitive. No production admission behavior has been
changed or enabled by these tests.

## Durable local observer checkpoint

The experimental observer now accepts only an authority-signed immediate
successor commitment binding this observer context, the predecessor revision/
digest and the next policy revision/digest. The complete next policy must have
its own valid authority signature and preserve existing pin/archive/cutoff rules.
Skipping a revision or presenting a successor for another retained head fails.
The same unchanged transition is an idempotent retry.

Before answering with a new head, the observer atomically flushes its encrypted
snapshot. It retains the current and immediately prior signed policy plus the
transition commitment. Different authenticated commitments at its current
revision produce persistent equivocation evidence and stop further observations
or advances. Random invalid signatures cannot trigger that fence. The public
status exposes the two signed commitments; no automatic conflict resolution is
implemented. A conflict at an earlier, no longer retained revision is refused
as stale input; this bounded store is not a complete historical transparency log.

The local state is capped at 2 MiB encrypted, with a shared reservation of twice
that size plus 8 KiB for replacement/ownership. Creation requires explicit mode,
a current signed genesis matching the independently pinned context and an
enrolled observer key. Ordinary reopen requires existing authenticated state.
Private buffers and mutable initialization inputs are captured before async
validation. Closure fences pending updates; an uncertain flush poisons the live
handle until a validated reopen. Reads refuse an expired installed policy under
the configured wall clock. Trusted clock behavior remains an explicit assumption.

The focused group passes 49 tests in four files, including 12 actual SIGKILL
boundaries across policy advance and conflict persistence, file/directory flush
failures, asynchronous close/input mutation, same-revision conflict, stale/mixed
votes and a certificate from four actual encrypted local observers. TypeScript
passes. The initial flush test failed to hook Node's named ESM import; it now uses
an explicit named-export fixture, and the separate child-process interruptions
exercise the actual filesystem path. Cross-platform observer CI is pending.

This adds no production listener, CLI activation or admission dependency. Reopening
a manually restored stale snapshot still cannot establish global freshness;
protected recovery, activation/distribution, clock/deadline integration and
membership/authority transitions remain required before enabling the role.

## Required next implementation and release gates

1. Local immediate successor authentication, persistence and current-revision
   conflict evidence are implemented above. Compose them with quorum activation
   and protected recovery; the local component alone does not complete freshness.
2. Protect observer state against stale backup/reinitialization and complete
   interruption, partition and concurrent-update tests. A new local file with an
   old observer key must not manufacture an empty history.
3. Add bounded authenticated transport, quorum activation, discovery/distribution
   and the monotonic caller deadline. Integrate fresh checks with issuance,
   redemption and wallet policy updates while preserving accepted work. Measure
   added traffic/latency and define behavior for expired/unreachable policies.
4. Implement sequential authority transitions authorized by old and new trust
   sets, explicit emergency distribution and evidence comparison. A compromised
   authority cannot repair itself by an unverified announcement. Membership
   changes need old-set fencing and new-set readiness, not a replacement list.
5. Finish archive migration beyond 64 configurations, same-key witness membership
   governance, observer recovery and independent operator/network validation.

The broad roadmap items remain open until these implementation and measurement
gates pass. Offline authority operation and volunteer-only observers remain the
constraints; no continuously operated central service is assumed.
