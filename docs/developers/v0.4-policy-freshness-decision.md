# Policy freshness: decision and first protocol boundary

## Current behavior and gap

A signed admission policy proves authority and content. Local revision retention
rejects rollback on that device; expiry bounds its use under the local clock.
None proves that another device has received a newer policy or that a partitioned
client has learned an emergency update. The existing policy store therefore does
not yet satisfy authenticated distributed freshness. This document preserves
that requirement rather than relabeling the existing expiry check as completion.

## Reference points

[TUF 1.0.36](https://theupdateframework.github.io/specification/v1.0.36/) separates
trusted root updates from fresh metadata: root changes advance sequentially and
need the prior and successor signature thresholds. Expiry bounds freeze attacks
under a trusted clock; it cannot force a hostile network to deliver an update.
We use these principles for the planned authority transition; this implementation
is not a TUF client and does not claim TUF compliance.

[Certificate Transparency](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) supplies
signed statements and consistency evidence. Comparing independently observed
views is still necessary to expose equivocation. A locally valid signed policy
alone cannot establish one globally shared view. The finite quorum analysis
below is our protocol analysis, not a claim made by either reference.

## Selected freshness contract

Keep policy authorization offline-capable. A separately pinned observer context
binds the community authority, initial policy revision/digest, and five volunteer
infrastructure keys/endpoints. Count four distinct current observations of the
same revision, policy digest and transition-chain digest. Each response signs a new random challenge, context
and a bounded request lifetime. No personal account, permit or credential appears
in this exchange. A caller must also authenticate the policy matching that digest
check its validity/cutoffs again before use, and enforce a monotonic operation
deadline. A signed observation cannot extend a policy expiry.

This can establish a head observed during that request. It cannot establish that
no later update exists, promise instantaneous revocation, or invalidate work
already accepted before an update. A new policy becomes globally effective only
after its activation is durably accepted by a quorum. One volunteer seeing a new
signature is insufficient. For overlapping activation/observation operations,
the guarantee must be stated relative to activation and request intervals.

The five keys represent a manually pinned trust set, not proof of five independent
operators. The selected fault assumption is at most one Byzantine observer,
with honest observers retaining monotone state and never approving two heads at
one revision. Both update activation and subsequent observation require four of
five. These quorums share at least three members, at least two honest under that
assumption. An honest retained update prevents an old-head observation quorum.

The finite model checks all 216 combinations of no/one faulty member, activation
quorums and observation quorums, plus conflicting same-revision approvals. It
retains counterexamples for three/three quorums, a locally seen unactivated update,
two honest observers losing their installed head, and replaying a prior observation.
Only six of the 32 reachability subsets contain four members; even those are not
sufficient if a reachable faulty member withholds a needed response. Independence,
schedules and latency require measurements, not subset counting.

## Implemented boundary

`admission-policy-freshness.ts` defines bounded observer contexts, requests, signed
votes and four/five-vote certificates. It binds the authority, genesis, member
keys/endpoints, exact challenge, time interval and expected revision/digest.
It rejects repeated signers, mixed heads, unknown signers, tampered signatures,
wrong contexts, expired/future requests, extra fields and invalid collections.
The 8 KiB certificate bound is independent of the full policy's larger byte limit.
The default request lifetime is three seconds, with a 30-second absolute maximum;
this is a protocol bound, not a measured Internet latency target.

Twenty-seven real-cryptography/finite-model tests pass in 526 ms. This is an
experimental protocol primitive. No production admission behavior has been
changed or enabled by these tests.

## Durable local observer checkpoint

The experimental observer now accepts only an authority-signed immediate
successor commitment binding this observer context, the predecessor revision/
digest and the next policy revision/digest. The complete next policy must have
its own valid authority signature and preserve existing pin/archive/cutoff rules.
Skipping a revision or presenting a successor for another retained head fails.
The same unchanged transition is an idempotent retry.

Before answering with a new head, the observer atomically flushes its encrypted
snapshot. It retains the current and immediately prior signed policy plus the
transition commitment. Different authenticated commitments at its current
revision produce persistent equivocation evidence and stop further observations
or advances. Random invalid signatures cannot trigger that fence. The public
status exposes the two signed commitments; no automatic conflict resolution is
implemented. A conflict at an earlier, no longer retained revision is refused
as stale input; this bounded store is not a complete historical transparency log.

The local state is capped at 2 MiB encrypted, with a shared reservation of twice
that size plus 8 KiB for replacement/ownership. Creation requires explicit mode,
a current signed genesis matching the independently pinned context and an
enrolled observer key. Ordinary reopen requires existing authenticated state.
Private buffers and mutable initialization inputs are captured before async
validation. Closure fences pending updates; an uncertain flush poisons the live
handle until a validated reopen. Reads refuse an expired installed policy under
the configured wall clock. Trusted clock behavior remains an explicit assumption.

The focused group passes 49 tests in four files, including 12 actual SIGKILL
boundaries across policy advance and conflict persistence, file/directory flush
failures, asynchronous close/input mutation, same-revision conflict, stale/mixed
votes and a certificate from four actual encrypted local observers. TypeScript
passes. The initial flush test failed to hook Node's named ESM import; it now uses
an explicit named-export fixture, and the separate child-process interruptions
exercise the actual filesystem path. Cross-platform observer CI is pending.

This adds no production listener, CLI activation or admission dependency. Reopening
a manually restored stale snapshot still cannot establish global freshness;
protected recovery, activation/distribution, clock/deadline integration and
membership/authority transitions remain required before enabling the role.

## Network collection and the contradictory-predecessor finding

The experimental loopback listener now handles one bounded request per connection,
with raw-connection/header/frame/lifetime limits and compression disabled. It
waits for already admitted asynchronous writes during idempotent shutdown.
Outbound connections enforce TLS, disable redirects/compression, bound replies,
terminate on completion/cancellation and have a standalone deadline. A collector
uses five pinned endpoints, accepts four matching member-bound replies, cancels
remaining work, checks its own monotonic deadline even if an adapter ignores
cancellation, and rechecks policy expiry before returning. No quorum reduction
occurs during a partition. An optional signed successor is installed before the
response, providing four durable local acknowledgements when collection succeeds.
A failed collection can leave accepted partial updates; exact retries complete
those without rollback. It is infrastructure-side code: personal-client policy
queries must use private routing before integration, rather than direct fan-out.

A new five-socket counterexample initially failed: two observers accepted one
revision-2 policy, two accepted a contradictory revision-2 policy, then each pair
accepted the same revision-3 content through different signed predecessors.
Comparing only the content digest incorrectly combined all four replies into
one certificate. No production admission path used this experimental component.

Heads now bind a transition-chain digest as well as revision and content digest.
The initial anchor is the independently pinned policy digest. Each later digest
hashes the observer context, complete preceding head and next revision/content.
The authority signs both full heads, observers preserve the exact predecessor,
and observations sign the chain digest. Collection groups replies by that chain;
equal content on contradictory branches is insufficient. Signing a later advance
requires its retained predecessor head, rather than reconstructing one from the
policy text. The reproduced failure is retained as a real-socket regression.
Old experimental snapshots lacking this field are refused, never silently reset.
Existing signed admission-policy v1/v2 formats remain unchanged.

proof that a restored snapshot is the newest one or a remedy for split branches.

## Replicated observer state and stale live owners

The protected experimental observer now reuses the retained history writer and
four authenticated current-head receipts. Each observer's history has a distinct
`policy-observer` role, a deterministic identifier derived from the complete
observer context and observer key, and the same five pinned volunteer keys.
Its context binds the complete observer configuration, not only the policy text.
Admission-spend configuration, enrollment, replay and restore reject this role.
An offline preparation returns one encrypted genesis that must be retained and
enrolled exactly; creating new random genesis ciphertext is not recovery.

The writer's existing encrypted intent and committed certificate are authoritative;
there is no second local snapshot that can lag an accepted update. Reopen checks
the retained signed/encrypted chain and its semantic transitions, including all
conflict evidence. A pending exact envelope remains the only retry after lost
replies or interruption. Historical policy expiry does not invalidate replay;
current expiry still prevents a live observation. The protected role uses full bounded snapshots. The first checkpoint supported
generation zero; explicit certified-generation restoration is described below.
Protected compaction remains open.

Every observation obtains four **fresh current-head** receipts before signing.
Startup readiness alone would not fence an already running clone of an old
backup. A clone that was open and previously ready now fails after other replicas
advance; an old certificate cannot substitute for the new request. Frozen replicas
also refuse ordinary writes. Closure, pending work, malformed/expired requests,
policy expiry and the monotonic request budget all prevent a late observation.
This adds infrastructure exchanges on every observation; latency, traffic and
power costs still need the resource evaluation before production integration.

A normal advance reserves space for one terminal conflict checkpoint. If an
operator later lowers the archive allocation, a newly detected conflict still
reaches the durable writer intent before archive replication may refuse. Reopen
retains that blocked pending state. Increasing available space permits completion;
no old usable state is substituted. An archive that cannot grow stops normal
advances rather than discarding retained evidence.

## Explicit reconstruction after owner loss

An offline policy-history replay now authenticates the original independent
genesis, every encrypted complete snapshot, immediate policy transitions and
every certified generation boundary. The selected predecessor must match the
complete final chain. Missing, reordered, duplicated, substituted or extra blocks,
wrong keys/pins, conflicting semantic snapshots, cancellation and byte/block/time
limits refuse the entire reconstruction. Expired historical policies remain
replayable; they still cannot authorize current observations. A contradiction
fence cannot disappear across generations, even in owner-signed snapshots.

Restore preparation binds that complete selected state to the exact new
destination, history context, original genesis and deterministic continuation.
The returned in-process capability cannot be forged by cloning its JSON review.
Creation persists an encrypted immutable restored baseline before opening the
new-generation writer. Later decisions remain exclusively in the writer's exact
committed/pending ciphertext. Reopen checks the baseline against its selected
proposal and then replays the current generation. It never substitutes the
initial baseline for later retained archive evidence. Explicit pristine writer
completion is refused if an archive already exists; this preserves evidence when
a previously used writer file disappears.

A continuation certificate is historical agreement, not live readiness. The
restored observer still obtains four fresh current-head acknowledgements before
observing or advancing. Previously active old-generation owners are fenced by
the replicated freeze. A recovered contradiction remains blocked after fresh
root replication and another restart. No helper silently creates a replacement
genesis, chooses new members, clears the old directory or bypasses the freeze.

The real-socket recovery fixture composes the existing freeze/download, agreement
and ciphertext write-back with certified child stores. It reconstructs through
two generations, preserves a partially stored update after actual replies are
lost, refuses an old owner's observation and retains conflict evidence. One
initial test incorrectly assumed three available peers had stored an update:
two immediate connection refusals can cancel the attempt before any write. The
fixture now loses replies only **after** actual durable storage and checks that
at least two replicas contain the partial tail before attempting reconstruction.
This partial-tail experiment uses honest test replicas; the durability guarantee
for acknowledged work remains four copies under the stated fault assumption.
Unreplicated evidence lost with its only owner cannot be recovered from the
network. Any still-available local evidence must be preserved and reconciled by
the future operator recovery workflow, never silently discarded.

The final focused group passes **47 tests in four files in 57.00 seconds**,
plus TypeScript. Sixteen actual process interruptions cover both usable and blocked restores at
baseline publication, writer/archive creation, root intent/index/certificate and
durable completion. An interruption before authoritative baseline publication
leaves the original files intact and uses a separately reviewed destination.
Later interruptions resume only the explicitly missing pristine component.
These tests do not measure physical power loss. The lightweight Windows/Linux/
macOS matrix is green at `1764906` for the preceding protected-observer boundary;
this reconstruction still requires its own complete CI gate.

The implementation exposes preparation/replay and restored-owner composition.
Production discovery, a resumable operator recovery command/UI, protected
compaction and private application integration remain open. Historical replay is
bounded to 10,001 blocks, 256 MiB total, 64 MiB per generation and at most 65
retained generations; reaching those bounds requires the pending compaction and
migration work rather than relaxing verification.

## Bounded current-head reads instead of repeated full writes

An observation previously reused the full checkpoint-write path for its fresh
four-copy check. That sent the complete encrypted snapshot again to current
replicas and persisted another local write certificate. The writer now exposes
an explicit current-head read. It creates a new challenge, validates four
member-bound signed current heads, rejects frozen/newer/divergent state and checks
its own monotonic deadline. A head response cannot be reinterpreted as a durable
write certificate. Empty or pending writer state is refused; initial readiness
and actual updates retain the original durable write path.

A replica already at the required head receives only the bounded head request.
A lagging replica receives the verified retained suffix, or an independently
certified compacted checkpoint, through that same target. Its final fresh exact
head can then count. A current compacted replica is not reinstalled merely to
perform prefix maintenance during a read. Outstanding work is cancelled after
four matches, and a transport that ignores cancellation cannot extend the caller's
deadline. The protected observer uses this read path for every observation; its
per-request policy expiry and challenge checks remain in place.

Real-socket application-byte experiments use generic opaque history payloads of
512 KiB and 2 MiB, not representative production policy files. In both cases five
head requests total **2,815 bytes**, and the four accepted responses total
**3,756 bytes**. The earlier full-write request path totals **3,503,255 bytes** and
**13,989,015 bytes**, respectively. Owner and replica manifest bytes remain
unchanged during the current-head read. These measurements omit TCP/TLS framing,
any unused fifth response and energy/CPU costs; they are not a complete network
or power budget. Repair still sends the ciphertext required by an outdated peer.

Eleven dedicated cases cover those measurements, exact returning-fourth repair,
replay/member substitution/signature failures, frozen/newer heads with and without
a retained archive, pending-state refusal, ignored cancellation, a delayed fourth
response, closure/serialization and compacted-prefix repair. The full focused
regression group passes **115 tests in eight files in 104.07 seconds**, plus
TypeScript. It includes writer/quorum/archive repair and protected observer
interruption/restoration. Cross-platform CI for this change remains pending.

## Resumable observer recovery library

`preparePolicyObserverRecovery` performs read-only discovery from independent
genesis pins and returns a capability bound to the workspace, selected ancestry,
barrier, view, key and available local state. A source loss must be declared
explicitly; a missing file or failed decryption is never treated as permission
to omit its evidence. Opening the reviewed workflow first checks that the local
observer has not changed. Pending updates/conflicts must obtain four durable
copies before recovery can continue. An existing committed write certificate
already establishes those copies, including for an older frozen generation.

The available source then saves a permanent encrypted recovery fence in its own
directory. It cannot advance or sign again, including after reopening. The fence
uses the source's captured storage authority and survives a subsequent workspace
publication failure. A review prepared before another accepted update is refused.
The workflow stores its local knowledge floor durably; reopening the workspace
never needs to discard it or declare the original source lost.

Explicit bounded calls advance freeze, actual agreement selection, ancestry
download, semantic verification, confirmation and provisioning. Before confirming
a new generation, replay must preserve the local floor and all policy transitions
and conflicts. A compromised owner signing a conflict-erasing checkpoint cannot
get this workflow to confirm or activate it. Each component's creation intent and
registration survive interruption. Missing/corrupt registered components stop
recovery rather than being recreated. Reopen/status perform no network work;
restoration remains a separate local review into a new directory, followed by a
separate observer open and fresh four-copy readiness.

Nine real-socket workflow cases cover phase resumption, changed/forged reviews,
explicit source loss, pending updates/conflicts, unavailable quorum, lost freeze
replies, damaged journals, semantic evidence deletion and cancellation of an
uncooperative transport. Thirteen SIGKILL cases cover source fencing, workspace
publication and each phase publication. A crash before workspace publication can
leave ambiguous encrypted candidates: tests retain them and use a separately
reviewed workspace. The combined protected observer/restoration/recovery group
passes **73 tests in six files in 104.61 seconds**. These are single-computer
simulations with real sockets, cryptography and process termination.

This is a callable operator library. Observer-specific CLI/desktop enrollment,
reviewed activation/distribution, protected compaction and private application
integration remain open. A local fence is not a global membership transition;
remote freeze/agreement supplies the independent generation barrier.

The three-OS matrix is green for 10cd0f3, including the corrected deadline-test
setup; full CI is green through 1764906. CI now cancels superseded runs on the
same pull request, retaining the latest run as the completion gate. It still
builds no native installers. CI for the recovery library remains pending.

   conflict evidence are implemented above. Compose them with quorum activation

2. Four-copy persistence, per-observation freshness and explicit selected-history
   reconstruction are implemented above. The resumable library workflow is implemented; complete its operator commands,
   reviewed activation and protected compaction; do not rebuild an empty history with an old key.
3. Bounded local transport, four-member collection/activation and the monotonic
   caller deadline are implemented above. Complete discovery/distribution,
   protected activation and private routing. Integrate fresh checks with issuance,
   redemption and wallet policy updates while preserving accepted work. Measure
   added traffic/latency and define behavior for expired/unreachable policies.
4. Implement sequential authority transitions authorized by old and new trust
   sets, explicit emergency distribution and evidence comparison. A compromised
   authority cannot repair itself by an unverified announcement. Membership
   changes need old-set fencing and new-set readiness, not a replacement list.
5. Finish archive migration beyond 64 configurations, same-key witness membership
   governance, observer recovery and independent operator/network validation.

## Private metadata query boundary (2026-10-18)

The private relay client can now request this metadata through the existing
two-hop encrypted transport. It generates the challenge after queued waiting and
route discovery, verifies the signed destination response and four-observer
certificate, and never calls the capability provider. The destination accepts
this operation only inside a decrypted private request, with ordinary rate,
owner-resource and bounded responder admission. It cannot select arbitrary
observer endpoints. An explicitly configured runtime observer supplies the
pinned context and current policy; observation sockets enter traffic accounting.
There is no background polling or direct fallback.

Real-socket integration uses five observers and 25 separate durable volunteer
stores on one computer. It rejects replayed challenges, stale gateway policy,
insufficient history copies, direct queries and owner-paused work. It also exposed
a shutdown bug: nested private admission previously escaped the tracked inbound
promise. The relay now awaits that work before closing its stores; a blocked
private publication survives shutdown and journal replay. These checks do not
establish independent operator availability. Bounded full-policy distribution and
issuer/relay enforcement remain separate gates.

A bounded query now carries only a caller-generated challenge/context/time window.
The reply carries an at-most-8-KiB four-observer certificate, without an account,
capability, caller-selected destination or full policy body. The carrier is not
trusted: the recipient verifies the independently pinned observer signatures and
exact original challenge. Matching an authenticated current policy and enforcing
a retained branch/revision remains the caller's responsibility.

The infrastructure collector can preserve a supplied challenge and clamps its
monotonic deadline to the caller's remaining lifetime. It snapshots request fields
before asynchronous verification, rejects wrong/expired contexts before dialing
and retains the existing at-most-five pinned fanout. A fixed-context responder
admits one collection by default (maximum two). Cancellation or timeout cannot
release its admission slot until outstanding adapters actually settle, including
an adapter that ignores cancellation; closing drains those retained operations.

The query deliberately does not transfer a full policy: signed policy v2 permits
512 KiB while one private payload permits 190 KiB. A bounded authenticated policy
transfer/migration path remains necessary; increasing the private frame cap or
falling back to direct personal-client connections is not an implicit solution.
No background polling, new cover traffic or default admission dependency is
enabled by these primitives.

observer collection pass. Cases cover challenge mutation/replay, substituted

### Desktop wallet enforcement

An explicit observer setup can now accompany a signed policy import. Its authority
and original policy head must match independently verified pins. Once installed,
those pins cannot be changed or removed through ordinary policy imports. Later
updates require an authority-signed immediate transition from the retained head;
an exact repeated import is safe after a lost local API reply.

The encrypted wallet retains installed and observed heads, including their
transition hashes. A newer four-observer head is written before reporting that
the installed policy needs updating. Older observations are then refused after
restart. A contradictory head at the same revision retains a permanent conflict
fence; ordinary imports cannot clear it. No tokens are reserved in these cases.

Every released operation obtains a new challenge through the private route within
its existing ten-second total budget. It checks the certificate before reservation
and rechecks retained policy state and both wall-clock and monotonic expiry before
transmission. Cancellation or failure during metadata lookup reports the actual
operation as not sent, unless a prior delivery attempt already made its outcome
uncertain. Holding a request performs no lookup. Direct clients cannot bypass the
wallet requirement. Lock/unlock retains pins and floors, without starting polling.

This is an optional personal-wallet gate, not network-wide instantaneous
revocation. Observations establish an exact head during their challenge window;
a later policy update can still race a transmitted operation. The relay and issuer
must enforce their own freshness requirements. Signed successors are currently
imported explicitly or through the bounded private update below; observer governance and independently protected client
backup recovery remain open. Restoring an old copy of the entire local wallet is
not equivalent to reopening its latest retained state.

Catch-up may retain an expired, signed immediate intermediate only when a higher
head has already been authenticated and retained. That intermediate cannot
authorize issuance, spending or retries. The final observed revision must be
current. The behavior survives restart and does not permit skipped transitions.

Validation: eight wallet cases cover fresh/replayed signatures, exact retries,
retained newer floors, signed transitions, permanent conflicts and disk failures.
Six real two-hop cases redeem blind tokens, retain held work, refuse old replies,
cancel metadata work, preserve the total deadline, refuse publication expiry before
reservation and reject direct bypass. The latter use controlled signed
observer responses; the separate five-observer/25-store test exercises protected
observer history. The desktop/API and existing route group passes 26 cases across
four files; TypeScript passes. These groups overlap earlier wallet checks.

The desktop now offers an explicit update that first collects a fresh head and
retains its floor, then downloads one signed immediate successor if needed. Each
request remains on the private route and uses no token. The action has a 25-second
outer cancellation bound; its observation and transfer each retain their existing
ten-second operation budget. Session lock or HTTP cancellation closes its clients.
At most one update runs per session, and it starts no periodic work. If several
revisions are missing, the UI shows the remaining count and permits another step.

An artifact contains one authority-signed policy and transition commitment.
64-KiB chunks produce replies below 90 KiB, fitting the existing private frame
limits. Requests bind the independently pinned context, exact predecessor,
artifact hash and index. The client admits at most four concurrent chunk requests
within one total operation deadline, cancels/drains the remaining workers on
failure, checks all sizes/indices/encodings and the complete digest, and finally
verifies the authority signature and transition before installation. A gateway's
own transfer hash cannot authenticate policy content.

The fixed-context responder retains at most two artifacts for 30 seconds, with
at most one preparation. Cancellation does not release that slot until the source
actually settles. Protected observers serve signed successors from their
authenticated current-generation archive without network repair. This is a
historical read, not a freshness claim. A restored baseline also supplies its
immediate predecessor's transition. Newly exported recovered configurations also
retain an explicit `archiveDirectory` pointing to the completed recovery download.
Older successors are read from that selected history only after full semantic
replay from the independently pinned original genesis. Its proposal must match
the continuation root; every encrypted block, transition and conflict fence is
checked before any result leaves the reader. Missing, corrupt, incomplete, owned
or conflicting archives fail closed without initialization, network repair or
temporary-file cleanup. Reads have a three-second monotonic bound, at most 10,001
blocks and 256 MiB; near-capacity availability within that bound is not established.
The current observer is checked again after replay, including cancellation and
shutdown. Desktop selection binds the canonical archive path and retains it in
storage accounting. Older configurations without this explicit path still open,
but cannot distribute older pre-restoration transitions; re-export verified
recovery configuration to enable that source. This does not compact history or
prove the availability of every old policy on an independent volunteer network.

Validation includes a real signed policy exceeding 190 KiB, malformed/reordered/
mixed chunks, a forged body with a recomputed transfer hash, cancellation,
monotonic cache expiry, exact retained history after restart, direct-path refusal,
owner controls, and full local HTTP update/lock/unlock. The four core transfer
cases, 30 source/observer/private transfer cases across three files, and 14
desktop-update/protected-query/wallet cases across three files pass. These groups
overlap earlier validation. TypeScript passes. Observer responses in the large
desktop-update test are controlled signatures; the separate protected-query suite
uses five observers with 25 actual durable stores.

## Explicit relay and witness enforcement

A configured relay can enroll the independently pinned public observer context
using `RELAY_ADMISSION_OBSERVERS_FILE` alongside the existing authority, policy and
storage-key files. Initial enrollment must match the context's genesis. The
encrypted policy store retains context, installed transition, highest observed
head and terminal conflicting quorum evidence. Omitting the context file on later
startup does not disable enforcement or select new observers. Installing another
revision requires `RELAY_ADMISSION_POLICY_ADVANCE_FILE` with its signed immediate
transition; unchanged policy needs no transition. The retained history-context
binding survives this policy-store format upgrade. Old readers reject the format.

Every cryptographically valid spend and exact retry obtains a new four-observer
head certificate before witness coordination or local spending. Invalid tokens
and already conflicting spends do not cause observer traffic. Configured witness
roles similarly authenticate the coordinator's request before collecting policy
evidence, including for an exact vote retry. Observation frames contain neither
the token nor the spend/request identifier. They contact only the retained
infrastructure endpoints and meter their sockets in the ordinary relay runtime.

Collection may learn a newer head without having its full policy. That floor is
durably recorded before refusal, so reopening with an older installed policy does
not resume admission. Conflicting same-revision quorum evidence remains fenced.
Each operation has one monotonic deadline, at most two admitted operations, and
at most two observation collections. Cancelled transports retain their collection
slot until actual settlement. Policy/date/branch checks run again around witness
coordination, durable spending and returned results. Work already durably reserved
when its deadline expires is retained; a refused response never refunds it.

This enables enforcement only for explicitly enrolled configurations. It does not
silently enroll existing relays, automate policy installation, provide independent
observer operators, or protect a legacy unpinned pilot. The public endpoints still
need the authenticated deployment described in the connection audit. Issuer
enforcement, protected compaction and authority/membership changes remain separate
gates. Delayed or partitioned quorums refuse admission; observations establish a
head within a bounded interval, not instantaneous global revocation.

## Issuer checks during explicit commands

An issuer ledger can independently enroll the same observer context while
installing its genesis policy. Its existing encrypted ledger atomically retains
the pins, transition and observed/conflict floors beside permit accounting; old
readers reject that additional state. Enrollment cannot be removed or replaced
by an ordinary policy import. Policy updates require signed immediate transitions.

`preparePolicyObservation()` creates one pending three-second challenge in memory.
The operator collects four matching votes for that exact request, then supplies
the reply to `grant(reply)` or `approve(permit, request, reply)`. Every call consumes
the pending challenge, including failed proof verification. Cached batch responses
also require a new proof. Challenges do not survive process close/reopen, and a
reply cannot authorize both a grant and approval. The operator may explicitly
cancel a pending challenge; this never refunds an allocated permit or bound batch.

The Node issuer command performs that exchange only for enrolled grant/issue
commands, using the ledger's retained endpoints. Init, status, policy installation
and retirement remain offline. There is no background polling or permanent issuer
service, and no recipient, permit, batch or token identifier is sent to observers.
These enrolled issuance commands do require temporary connectivity to a quorum;
they cannot promise fully offline issuance of a demonstrably current policy.

Expiry or shutdown during signing preserves the already reserved batch and refuses
late output. Recovery requires that exact batch plus a fresh observation. Newer
or conflicting quorum evidence is flushed before refusal; installing an ordinary
older policy cannot resume issuance. This does not make permits Sybil-resistant
or distribute one private signing key among operators.

Seventeen issuer freshness/ledger/retirement tests pass. An additional subprocess
test runs the actual issuer command against five protected observers backed by
25 local durable histories: enrollment, grant, newer-head refusal with no signing
reservation, restart, exact signed update, successful unblinding, cached recovery,
and refusal after two replicas per observer disappear. These are local processes,
not independent operators or an Internet availability measurement.
