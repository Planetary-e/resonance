# Preparing a frozen volunteer history

Status: **experimental recovery preparation, 2026-10-15**. This extends
[authenticated checkpoint exchange](v0.4-history-checkpoint-exchange.md).
It freezes an enrolled generation and collects complete encrypted evidence.
It does **not** allocate or activate another writer, perform quorum write-back,
restore application spend state, or reopen damaged admission histories.
The follow-up [successor reservation](v0.4-history-checkpoint-successor.md)
implements selected-history write-back and exclusive durable commitments, with
a documented blocking case for competing proposals; activation remains pending.

## API and ordering

`createHistoryCheckpointRecovery`, exported by `@resonance/relay`, requires the
independently retained context, root digest, current generation, owner signing
key, local encryption key, and all five pinned member identities/endpoints.
`nextGeneration` is an owner-requested barrier greater than the current
generation. Supplying a number does not reserve exclusive ownership of that
generation. Initialization is explicit; missing state on reopen is an error.

Calling `prepare()`:

1. Signs a fresh owner freeze command and atomically persists its intent before
   contacting any member. A previous completed preparation remains retained.
2. Sends owner-signed freeze requests to the five pinned members with one fresh
   challenge and validity window. Each request binds the enrolment root and
   recipient; its `sequence` field carries the requested successor barrier.
   The frame also carries a separately signed `HistoryFreeze` matching the
   context, generations and challenge.
3. Each honest member flushes the one-way barrier before signing its frozen
   head. Existing blocks remain intact. Append operations, including exact
   retries of already stored data, are refused after the barrier is installed.
   Owner pauses on new storage do not block this retention operation.
4. For each frozen member, fetches every block from the pinned root through its
   advertised head. Requests and replies are authenticated and target-bound.
   Each block must have the correct owner signature, context, generation,
   sequence and predecessor. Every read must retain that member's frozen head
   and requested barrier.
5. Requires four distinct members with complete validated histories. A member
   that signs a head but withholds a block does not count. A fifth silent,
   invalid or incomplete member can be excluded. Histories must be compatible
   prefixes; the longest collected chain is selected, including valid partial
   updates. Collected authenticated forks cause refusal. Any previously saved
   preparation must also be a prefix of the new result; local retries cannot
   regress when the only holder of a previously observed partial tail disappears.
6. Validates the four frozen head anchors against that complete selected chain
   and atomically persists the preparation before returning it. Outstanding
   exchanges are cancelled. Close, expiry or an uncertain local flush prevents
   a successful return.

Members are frozen and read concurrently. A member's own barrier precedes its
reads; selection waits for four complete frozen histories. Under the existing
correct-owner / at-most-one-Byzantine-member assumptions, four frozen members
intersect a completed four-member write in honest retained evidence. This is
the preparation portion of the [required ordering](v0.4-history-checkpoint-decision.md#required-ordering-for-a-future-implementation),
not a proof of a complete repeated-recovery protocol.

The output is a `HistoryRecoveryPreparation` with kind `frozen-history`, four
signed freeze replies and the entire selected owner-signed encrypted chain.
`parseHistoryRecoveryPreparation` validates historical evidence, distinct
same-attempt members, every chain link and all shorter frozen-head anchors.
It does not claim that four replicas already hold the longest selected suffix.
Durable write-back remains mandatory before operational recovery succeeds.

## Failure and restart behavior

- Failure may already have frozen some or all members. It never thaws them or
  deletes their data. The persisted intent records that possibility.
- Reopening sends nothing. `read()` exposes historical intent/preparation;
  it cannot authorize a writer. Explicit `prepare()` performs another round
  with a fresh challenge; old replies cannot satisfy it.
- Repeating the same barrier is safe even when a member retains an earlier
  freeze command. The new signed response binds the fresh request and current
  local head. The old command's challenge is not treated as freshness.
- A higher barrier is never lowered. If it appears during collection, that
  member cannot satisfy the lower attempt. A higher barrier outside the selected
  quorum is not excluded by this protocol; preparation grants no exclusive
  authority. Two recovery directories may prepare the same barrier, but neither
  receives permission to write.
- I/O failures poison the affected local handle until reopen. SIGKILL can leave
  an intent or a completed preparation; neither resumes writes. Full rollback to
  an older valid encrypted snapshot remains undetectable without external
  anchors or further quorum evidence.

## Bounds and deployment limits

- One active attempt per handle, at most five concurrent exchanges, one block
  per read and no unbounded history response.
- Default and maximum deadline: 30 seconds; minimum: 50 ms. Both monotonic time
  and signed wall-clock expiry are checked. A complete chain that cannot be
  fetched/verified within that budget fails closed. Resumable large-history
  transfer is not implemented.
- At most 10,001 blocks and 64 MiB of serialized encrypted envelopes per member
  chain. The encrypted coordinator file is limited to 96 MiB, plus its temporary
  atomic replacement. Parallel chains, parsing and encryption create additional
  bounded memory copies; these limits are not peak RSS guarantees.
- Existing frame and connection limits apply. The dedicated listener remains
  loopback-only; public endpoints require separately configured WSS. This is not
  an Internet bootstrap or an admission bypass.
- Fixed membership, at most one Byzantine member, an uncompromised non-forking
  owner, retained keys/pins and durable honest replica state remain assumptions.
  Conflicting collected evidence is detected; omitted members and independent
  volunteer ownership cannot be verified by this round.
- Payloads remain opaque ciphertext. Decryption and validation of actual spend
  and permanent-denial semantics belong to the future reviewed restore path.

## Validation

```sh
npm run build
npx vitest run --maxWorkers=1 history-checkpoint history-exchange
npm test -- --maxWorkers=1
```

New coverage includes barrier/challenge binding, full-chain and shorter-prefix
validation, incomplete/forged replies, sequence limits, lost replies and fresh
retries, retained partial tails, collected forks, local regression, failed
intent/barrier/preparation flushes, deadlines, close, racing old writes,
superseding barriers, and missing/mismatched pins. Real loopback sockets cover
collection, persistent barriers after volunteer restart and SIGKILL of
pending/prepared recovery processes.

Local validation: TypeScript compilation passes; the three focused protocol,
recovery and socket files pass **41 tests** in 45.56 seconds. The complete suite
passes **121 files / 691 tests** in 357.62 seconds with one worker, including
20 tests added for recovery preparation. No production source changed during
that full run. Remote validation is recorded separately in draft PR #34.

The preceding dependency correction updates Sharp to 0.35.5 and its bundled
libvips packages to address [GHSA-wq5f-xc86-pv6w](https://github.com/lovell/sharp/security/advisories/GHSA-wq5f-xc86-pv6w).
Production audit, SVG conversion, Transformers import and the quick evaluation's
44 metrics passed locally. [Linux CI for that correction](https://github.com/Planetary-e/resonance/actions/runs/38704437633)
passed audit, compilation, all 120 existing test files and the quick evaluation.
That run precedes the recovery changes and does not certify them.

These are controlled single-host tests. Power loss, arbitrary concurrent recovery
schedules, Internet deployment and operational restoration remain unverified.
Windows retains the existing file-fsync limitation without directory-fsync.

## Next implementation boundary

Selected-history write-back and immutable successor reservations are implemented
in the [next stage](v0.4-history-checkpoint-successor.md). Resolve its split-vote
blocking case before enabling operational recovery. Define certificate-controlled
activation together with cross-generation ancestry and durable transition
evidence. Add bounded resumable download for histories exceeding one attempt's
deadline, live lagging-suffix repair,
mandatory checkpoint replication before protected admission outputs,
semantic validation and reviewed atomic local installation. Generation numbers
and four freeze signatures alone are insufficient.
