# Signed successor agreement on durable volunteer stores

Status: **experimental network agreement, history transfer and generation provisioning,
2026-10-16**. This implements the signed messages and durable transitions selected
by the [view-change evaluation](v0.4-successor-view-change-decision.md), including
bounded network exchange, explicit continuation after owner restart and opt-in
selected-history download/write-back and explicit certificate-controlled local
generation activation. [Remote provisioning](v0.4-history-provisioning.md) now
creates a resumable child in a locally enrolled predecessor slot. Cross-generation
owner download and live admission restore remain pending.

## What changes

The earlier [one-shot reservation](v0.4-history-checkpoint-successor.md) can split
two honest volunteers for A and two for B. A dishonest fifth signing both leaves
each proposal with three votes. Those irrevocable reservations still cannot be
reset. The new protocol separates a tentative preparation from confirmation,
allowing a later round to resolve tentative choices using verifiable evidence.

The membership remains five pinned volunteers and the quorum remains four. A
view is a recovery round for the same context, root and predecessor/successor
generation pair. It does not change membership or create a new writer generation.
Safety assumes at most one Byzantine volunteer, retained honest state and no
duplicated honest signing identities. Progress additionally needs a stable
proposer, available authentic selected ciphertext, four volunteers with compatible
retained prefixes, enough storage, and enough time to finish the bounded attempt.
A volunteer with a longer or divergent accepted tail refuses a shorter/different
selection. This can block progress; the protocol never solves it by truncation.

## Authenticated evidence

`@resonance/core/history-agreement` defines separately versioned/domain-separated
v2 requests, reports, preparation votes and confirmation votes. Every request is
owner-signed and binds the target volunteer, slot, view, challenge, validity
window, exact proposal identity and exact proof digest. Every reply is signed by
the pinned target. Requests last at most 30 seconds; historical evidence remains
valid after that window. Expiry cannot revoke an issued vote.

- **Promise:** durably enter a view and report the highest preparation certificate
  retained before an earlier confirmation. Four distinct same-attempt reports
  form a view certificate. Same-view retries retain the original entry snapshot.
- **Prepare:** verify four view reports. Preserve the proposal from their highest
  valid prepared proof, or select a valid proposal if none exists. Conflicting
  highest proofs refuse work. Verify every selected local ciphertext block and
  persist one proposal choice in the current view before returning a vote.
- **Confirm:** require the matching four-member preparation certificate and the
  volunteer's own preparation in the still-current view. Persist that complete
  proof before returning confirmation. Four matching confirmations decide the
  proposal even when the replies never reach the owner.
- **Advance:** a higher promise clears only the current tentative preparation;
  it preserves the proof behind the highest previous confirmation. Older
  preparation and confirmation requests are refused thereafter.

The candidate value reuses the immutable owner-signed successor proposal. Its
identity remains unchanged across views. Legacy `sealed` receipts cannot serve
as either new phase. Vote/report collections require four distinct members from
one exact attempt, and parsers canonicalize member order before hashing proofs.
Public hash/selection helpers expect already parsed evidence.

## Persistence and compatibility

`createHistoryAgreementReplica` is the replica entry point over the existing
checkpoint store. The store saves the agreement in its encrypted manifest, using
the same lock and atomic file/fsync/rename/directory-flush sequence as the payload
index and freeze barrier. Signed responses remain local until persistence
completes. A final expiry check can suppress a response without undoing the
persisted transition. Uncertain I/O failures poison the handle until reopen.

Reopen authenticates retained requests, reports, votes and certificates, checks
their phases, proposal relationships, context/member/root/generation pins and
complete selected ancestry, then loads the indexed ciphertext blocks. Retained
state includes the entry report, the current tentative vote and the highest
confirmed prepared proof; it does not grow an unbounded chain of earlier views.

Opt-in is deliberately narrow:

1. A valid first promise may opt in an already frozen store with **no legacy
   successor reservation**. The opt-in and view barrier are one manifest write.
2. A store with any legacy reservation refuses opt-in, preserving that exact
   reservation. Four old vote reports do not justify resetting it.
3. After opt-in, legacy sealing and repair are refused, as are changes to the
   successor generation. Ordinary writes remain frozen. Same-barrier freeze
   requests and reads remain available.
4. Unmodified older manifests remain readable. Older binaries reject the added
   manifest fields, preventing silent downgrade of an opted-in store.

This fences the two protocols at each honest signer. It does not make an existing
split set of legacy reservations recoverable. Four legacy seals and four new
confirmations cannot coexist under these honest-state rules: their signer sets
intersect in honest volunteers that refuse participation in both protocols.

Agreement frames are bounded to 256 KiB, view certificates to 192 KiB, individual reports
to 64 KiB and vote certificates to 16 KiB. Existing proposal, manifest and storage
bounds still apply. Views range from 1 to 1,000,000 and never wrap. An optional
independent `minimum.agreementView` anchor detects rollback to an earlier view.
It does not detect erased votes within the same view; deliberate deletion,
copied signing identities and complete rollback without an adequate external
anchor remain outside the guarantee. SIGKILL is not a physical power-loss test;
Windows retains the existing limitation of file flushes without directory fsync.

## Network exchange and owner continuation

`listenHistoryAgreementReplica` exposes one request per WebSocket connection at
`/history-agreement`, on loopback only. Remote use requires a separately configured
TLS proxy and a secure endpoint. The listener rejects binary, malformed and
oversized messages, refuses additional messages on a used connection, caps HTTP
headers at 4 KiB and disables compression. Its default four-connection limit can
be configured up to eight. The default ten-second connection deadline includes
pre-handshake idle time and is bounded to at most 30 seconds.

`exchangeHistoryAgreement` sends at most 256 KiB and accepts at most 64 KiB for a
view report or 4 KiB for a vote. It refuses insecure remote endpoints, mismatched
targets and redirects. An AbortSignal bounds pending connections, including a
silent established peer; an independent 30-second transport deadline also applies.
An optional socket callback supports transport accounting. The owner authenticates
each returned reply against its exact signed request, including phase, target,
challenge and proof digest. Old replies cannot count in a fresh collection.

`createHistoryAgreementCoordinator` pins the owner, context, root, predecessor,
successor and five member keys. Its endpoints must be distinct and satisfy the
secure transport policy. This does not establish independent machines/operators.
Initialization takes an explicit valid proposal and a view (default 1); reopening
requires the existing encrypted file. Nothing is sent during construction or read.

The `history-agreement-owner.json` snapshot is bounded to 2 MiB and stores the
preferred candidate, current view/phase, exact selected proposal, complete view
and preparation proofs, final certificate when available, and current attempt.
It uses flushed atomic replacement and a single-writer lock. Reopen verifies all
signatures and phase/slot/proof relationships. Missing, damaged or mismatched
state refuses startup. `minimumView` can enforce an independently retained lower
bound; full rollback without such evidence remains undetectable.

The explicit `run(signal?)` operation:

1. Persists a fresh challenge and time window **before** each pending phase sends
   any requests, including prerequisites for a volunteer that missed a phase.
2. Collects four current matching reports and persists the selected proposal and
   complete view certificate before any preparation request.
3. When `historyTransfer: {}` is enabled, downloads and verifies the selected full
   chain, then copies the missing suffix to each candidate voter before preparation.
   `historyTransfer.transport` can supply a custom data transport. Omission retains
   the agreement primitive that requires history to be already present.
4. Collects and persists four preparation votes before any confirmation request.
5. Collects and persists four confirmations before reporting success.

Each collection has five member jobs and finishes on four valid replies, cancelling
the unused connections. A different fourth volunteer can participate in each phase:
the coordinator first supplies its missing promise/preparation prerequisites.
It uses the stored certificates to determine which prerequisites were already
durably acknowledged. Failures never count as acknowledgements or authorize
lowering the quorum.

One wall-clock and monotonic deadline covers the entire run, including signing,
verification, all phases, prerequisite requests and fsync. It defaults to 30 seconds
and can be configured from 50 ms to 30 seconds. Close, abort and timeout cancel
pending work; late replies cannot change the saved phase. JavaScript cannot
preempt synchronous work, so checks also follow expensive verification and writes.
An overrun during the final flush suppresses success but retains the decision.
The whole-run and collection timers report expiry consistently even when scheduled
for the same instant; a deterministic regression check covers that ordering.

After restart or a failed attempt, `run()` resumes the persisted phase with fresh
requests and the same selection/proofs. A retained final certificate is returned
without traffic; it is historical evidence, not a claim of current availability.
`beginView(higherView)` is a separate explicit operation: it persists the higher
round and last locally known prepared proof without sending or erasing volunteer
votes. A subsequent `run()` collects fresh reports and follows their highest
prepared evidence, even if that differs from the owner's preferred candidate.
No timeout automatically raises a view, no concurrent run is allowed, and a
decided owner cannot reset its decision. Discovering a higher remote view and
arbitrating competing coordinators are not automated here.

## Complete-history download and write-back

The owner selects the proposal **after** four view reports, including a higher
prepared proposal that differs from its preference. `historyTransfer: {}` then
uses `/history-agreement-data` on the same pinned transport origin to retrieve
ciphertext by sequence and exact digest. A separately domain-separated owner
signature binds the data action, requested digest and the signed preparation
authorization. That authorization binds the target, slot, view, fresh challenge,
expiry, proposal and view certificate. Member-signed responses bind the exact data
request. Head/read/stage acknowledgements cannot count as agreement votes.

Download walks backwards from the selected, signed head to the pinned root. For
each block, the owner tries all five pinned sources and uses the first authentic
matching response, cancelling the rest. It verifies predecessor links and all
four original freeze anchors before uploading anything. One dishonest source
cannot substitute a block, and a missing/invalid source cannot supply a vote.
The cache is bounded to 10,001 blocks / 64 MiB of encoded ciphertext and exists
only for the current run. Restart downloads it again using fresh authorization;
resumable owner-side download is still pending. The existing durable owner phase,
selected proposal and proofs are the transfer intent; no new selection is inferred
from a partial download.

For each prospective voter, the owner checks a signed head against the verified
chain. A newer or incompatible accepted head is refused. Only the missing suffix
is sent, newest block first. Every new block must match the digest selected by the
proposal or the predecessor digest of the last staged block. This backwards order
prevents unrelated intermediate blocks from becoming accepted history merely
because the owner signed them.

The recipient stores incomplete ciphertext under a separate `transfer` index in
its encrypted manifest. It retains one pending candidate in the current promised
view, uses the existing single-writer lock, and counts both accepted and staged
blocks against its storage limits. Each block file is flushed before its staging
index. Reopen authenticates the authorization and every descending link; missing
or corrupt staged files fail closed. Ordinary reads and agreement votes still see
only the accepted index.

When the suffix reaches the current accepted head, the recipient verifies the
join and every frozen-head anchor, then publishes the complete index in one
flushed manifest replacement. Files precede the index. A crash therefore retains
either the previous accepted prefix plus pending staging, or the complete chain;
it never exposes a partial suffix. Disk errors poison the handle until reopen.
Lost acknowledgements are idempotent, and a signed pending position lets a fresh
attempt resume at the next missing predecessor. A higher promised view may clear
only this uncommitted staging. It preserves all accepted blocks and retained
agreement proofs; same-view attempts cannot replace a pending candidate.

A prepared or confirmed chain cannot be extended by this transfer path. Legacy
reservations still prohibit provisional agreement and staging. Data reads remain
available for authentic retained ciphertext without converting old seals to votes.
The final prepare/confirm checks still require the exact complete accepted chain.
A receiver that cannot join the candidate retains its original history and refuses
to vote, even if four other volunteers can decide without it.

Data and agreement use separate WebSocket payload limits: data requests allow one
maximum encrypted envelope plus one maximum preparation frame and 4 KiB overhead;
read responses allow one maximum envelope plus 4 KiB; head/stage responses allow
4 KiB. They share the listener's connection cap, handshake/connection deadlines,
no-compression policy and one-message rule. The data client accepts the standard
`/history-agreement` endpoint and derives `/history-agreement-data` on that origin;
remote deployments must route both paths through their configured TLS proxy.

The original whole-run deadline includes download, verification, staging, disk
flushes and votes. A transport ignoring cancellation cannot hold the coordinator
open indefinitely or deliver a late state transition. Large histories can exceed
the 30-second run budget: recipient staging resumes, but download currently starts
again. These bounded tests demonstrate progress for available compatible histories,
not unconditional liveness for every retained-tail combination or Internet timing.

### Repeated-proof verification and the remaining CI timeout

[Linux CI for `855cefe`](https://github.com/Planetary-e/resonance/actions/runs/38762984372)
passed those five corrected cases but failed one of 778 tests: carrying a hidden
decision into a later view while repairing a different fourth member exhausted
the **production 30-second whole-run deadline**. Audit and TypeScript compilation
passed. This failure differs from the earlier aggregate test-budget failures.

Each transferred block carries a view certificate, whose reports may repeat
the same proposal, freeze receipts and prepared proof. Re-parsing these nested
objects repeatedly performed the same Ed25519 checks in the owner and replicas.
The history protocol now memoizes only successful cryptographic verification:

- A process-local LRU holds at most 1,024 SHA-256 digests of the complete message,
  signature and public key. It retains no payload or mutable input references.
- A change to any authenticated byte requires verification again. Failed checks
  are never cached. Eviction and process restart only discard this optimization.
- Message shape, context, target, phase, quorum membership, expiry and durable
  state are checked on every operation. An old signature still verifies, but an
  expired request or stale view is still refused. No authorization is cached.
- This optimization is confined to history signatures. Wire formats, quorum
  requirements, deadlines, durability and generation barriers are unchanged.

On the same local machine, the isolated carried-decision test fell from **13.46
seconds to 1.34 seconds** (test-body time, one run each). This is a local diagnostic,
not a guaranteed latency bound. All 29 focused history/cache tests pass. An

proposal. It must carry the earlier decision, copy missing ciphertext to the new
fourth member, and preserve its confirmation after SIGKILL/reopen. Each process
has its own verification cache; there is no shared owner/replica cache shortcut.

## Certified generation continuation

`createHistoryContinuationRoot(proposal, confirmedCertificate, context)` creates
a version-2 `continuation` root. Four authentic confirmations must decide the
exact proposal and predecessor slot. A prepared certificate, legacy reservation,
different proposal or altered generation cannot authorize it.

The root ID derives from the decided proposal ID in a separate domain. There is
no new owner-selected nonce, ciphertext or replacement snapshot. Different valid
confirmation proofs for the same proposal, including later views, produce the
same root ID. These proofs are interchangeable evidence of the same boundary;
the first durable representation may be retained. Parsers always authenticate
the complete proof before trusting the ID. Proofs are bounded to 36 KiB.

The proposal already binds the parent root, generation, exact last sequence and
last block ID. Its `nextGeneration` becomes the continuation's generation. The
new generation starts at sequence zero with this boundary, then ordinary encrypted
deltas append at sequence one. A continuation has **no standalone plaintext**:
`openHistoryCheckpoint` refuses to treat it as a restored snapshot. The retained
parent chain is required. Ordinary encrypted sequence-zero roots now qualify
only for genesis (generation zero).

### Replica and writer gates

`store.activateContinuation(root)` requires the exact complete selected parent
chain and matching freeze, rejects legacy reservations and incomplete transfers,
and atomically persists the continuation before returning. It never removes old
blocks, clears a freeze or replaces a previously activated value. Later agreement
traffic cannot change that parent slot. The same root is an idempotent retry;
equivalent newer confirmation evidence does not rewrite the saved boundary.

Opening a child store requires both the certified root and its retained parent
store handle, on creation **and reopen**. The parent verifies its own durable
activation and the replica binding. Each parent was itself opened with its parent,
so the chain of generations is checked back to genesis. Missing authority,
rollback to a pre-activation manifest, missing/corrupt predecessor ciphertext or
an incompatible retained tail refuses opening. There is no automatic reset.
Ancestry is capped at 64 links and 256 MiB in aggregate, counting retained blocks
and two maximum manifest copies per generation. Each store's own capacity still
applies. A generation change cannot multiply the storage budget without bound.

A non-genesis checkpoint writer also persists its continuation proof and checks
it on reopen. It must first replicate the root and obtain four fresh durable write
receipts before appending any delta. A historical decision certificate alone is
not current readiness. Old-generation writes remain refused by the old freeze.

### Validation and operational boundary

The continuation tests use real signatures and encrypted stores. They cover
equivalent later-view proofs, wrong phases/proposals/generations, insufficient
signers, absent parent authority, caller mutation, exact retries, retained longer
tails and legacy seals, parent rollback/corruption and failed fsync. Three SIGKILL
tests interrupt activation before rename, after rename and after durable commit,
before an acknowledgement reaches the caller. A real-socket test writes through
two consecutive certified generations with four volunteers and an unavailable
fifth, then reopens their ancestry and checks that the original history remains.
The history regression group passed **181 tests** before the final ancestry-budget
case; the final continuation group passed **11 tests**. TypeScript compilation
and the complete suite passed **135 files / 793 tests** in **418.91 seconds**, with
one worker, including the listener-startup correction. All 324 TypeScript source
hashes were unchanged during that complete run. A subsequent test-only refinement
binds reply interception to both the entry address and port; all **eight private-client
tests** passed again in **41.53 seconds**. Production sources are unchanged after
that full run.

These are explicit library APIs. The original continuation integration test
provisions child stores locally. The subsequent
[remote provisioning milestone](v0.4-history-provisioning.md) adds a durable
creation intent and unpublished staging directory, allowing a signed remote
request to finish an interrupted child creation safely. Ordinary store enrolment
still refuses partial directories; only the provisioner's specifically registered,
unpublished construction can resume. Current-generation history transfer still
verifies/copies that generation; the child store separately requires its already
retained ancestors. Owner-side download across generation roots and live admission
restoration remain pending. The CI for commit `8f54bba` passed before that follow-up.
