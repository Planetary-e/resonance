# v0.4 bounded architecture study — October 18, 2026

## Decision

**No-go for an implementation prototype under the unchanged joint requirements.**
Exactly three candidates were assessed below. None clears the premises needed
for a meaningful prototype. This rejects these configurations, not the entire
volunteer-only product vision. All [K1–K8 requirements](v0.4-acceptance-kpis.md)
remain mandatory and unqualified.

The owner authorized this study with additional account-usage and eight aggregate
active-hour ceilings, and at most three designs. The study ends at this
three-candidate cut. Prior public-transform, two-hop-collusion and at-least-four
latency alternatives retain their histories; no counter resets. No protocol,
traffic mode, installer or production configuration was changed.

## Subsequent owner-approved design requirements — October 18

These decisions define the direction for candidate 2. They do not establish a
working protocol, approve production activation, reset the study limits or close
any K1–K8 gate. The implementation-prototype no-go above remains in force until
the unresolved premises and feasibility have been reviewed.

### Transport trust

Evaluation may assume an honest, independently operated mix between entry and
exit, targeting collusion of entry and exit under that assumption. This is a
conditional security target, not demonstrated protection. Count actual operator
control across roles; different devices, domains or keys do not establish
independence. Evidence of independence, compute-role overlap and the full
collusion boundary still require an explicit policy. Timing, cross-session and
active-observer attacks remain within the existing acceptance contract.

### Search outputs and consent

Search should return possible-match notices and opaque contact references scoped
to the query and recipient, replacing stable publication identifiers in results.
Do not expose exact similarity scores, fingerprints/embeddings, original texts
or root accounts. Both parties must independently consent to opening a private
relationship, then choose what text or contact data to disclose using a fresh
relationship identity. No new result-count limit is approved here.

Existence, count, ordering, chosen queries and cross-session inference remain
part of K5/K6 evaluation. Calling an output authorized does not exempt unintended
inference from those tests. This requirement is not yet implemented or validated.

### Eligibility and bounded issuance

The owner accepted eligibility claims with a fixed allowance per entitlement
period as the basic abuse-control direction. One claim must not gain another
allowance through 100 identities or by moving between relays. Exact retries reuse
the existing decision without a second debit. Anonymous, single-use operation
tokens should separate entitlement checking from activity; neither token
unlinkability nor safe distributed spend enforcement is assumed from this
decision alone.

Community attesters would have bounded, auditable issuance budgets. Creating
additional keys or communities must not create budget. Admitting an attester or
increasing its budget needs a verifiable federation decision; its governance and
protocol remain unselected. Issued rights must remain usable during their validity
without a continuously online issuer, subject to current authorization and usable
quorum. No fallback may create fresh quota when authoritative state is unavailable.

Per-claim and community budgets do **not** establish one claim per person across
communities. Independently adjudicated eligibility, additional-claim acquisition
cost, quota amplification and false exclusion must be evaluated before admission
can pass. Budget exhaustion or manual invitations must not silently redefine the
eligible population to improve K4's automation/fairness results. Allowance sizes,
the eligibility evidence source and cross-community abuse thresholds are not
selected by this approval. No government ID, biometric, payment or central
identity registry requirement is introduced.

K4 retains its existing thresholds and corpus requirements. Before an experiment,
declare how independent claims are obtained and labeled, the adversary's issuance
control, and a measurable acceptable amplification/cost boundary. If the evidence
does not clear the agreed limits, stop for an owner decision under the existing
stop rules; successful token plumbing cannot close eligibility. No additional
experiment or implementation iteration begins solely from this decision record.

Under a subsequent, separately authorized implementation tranche, the first
[local claim-bound permit component](v0.4-completion-plan.md#october-18-durable-operator-claim-permit-grants)
has been implemented and tested. It binds manually approved opaque references to
one recoverable permit within a single retained issuer ledger. This does not
supply the missing eligibility source, federation budget authority or distributed
claim enforcement, and does not lift the confidential-search prototype no-go.

## Common adversary and safety contract

Retain five pinned durability members, four confirmations and at most one
Byzantine member. Declare client-link observers, cooperating transport operators,
cross-session observers, multiple-domain Sybils and eligible chosen-query users
separately. Domain counts do not prove operator independence. Four storage
receipts alone prove neither confidential computation nor honest computation.

K5 must distinguish intentionally authorized search results from unauthorized
access to publication representations. Before further evaluation, freeze who can
see sketches, issue queries and receive which results. An authorized result is
not itself a privacy breach; dictionary identification from a representation
outside that authorization is the relevant candidate-1 attack below.

Admission needs independently adjudicated eligibility, a fixed entitlement period,
and an explicit cost for acquiring additional independent claims. One signed claim
can receive one allowance across 100 identities; this does not establish one claim
per person. Anonymous tokens do not supply eligibility: [RFC 9576 §§3.5–3.6](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9576)
makes attestation deployment-specific. All candidates retain this unresolved K4
dependency and must survive issuer loss without a permanent issuer service.

Every candidate must retain spends, withdrawal tombstones, policy cutoffs and
conflict evidence across authorized membership changes. Successor activation needs
authenticated retained state and funded storage before necessary old members
retire. Missing quorum means refusal; historical receipts cannot establish current
freshness. Safe replacement, archive-boundary migration and compaction remain
uncompleted K2/K3 work, not assumed services.

## Three candidate architectures

### 1. Community-keyed semantic sketches — reject

Clients embed locally, apply a secret scope/epoch transform, and send searchable
sketches through the existing entry/destination route. Destination ranking feeds
encrypted match confirmation. Protected admission/history remains authoritative.

**Assumptions:** scope members receive the transform key; untrusted relays do not.
If an enrolled adversary can also obtain target sketches, it can transform 100
known candidate texts locally and compare them. Network quota cannot limit that
local enumeration. If sketches are inaccessible to members, the design instead
needs an explicit access/output model and analysis of member–relay collusion.

**Path:** embedding → current policy/admission → entry → destination ranking or
mutation → required witness/history commits → response. Key/entitlement renewal
adds issuance dependencies. Existing protected-path cost remains.

**Falsifier:** an allowed key-holder/sketch-observer identifies more than 5% of
targets, or the unchanged sparse/colluding transport exceeds K6. Ordinary PRF
tags do not preserve semantic distance; [RFC 9497](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9497)
specifies a two-party primitive, not a threshold semantic-search solution.
This design cannot clear the joint requirements.

### 2. Malicious-secure confidential search on volunteer shares — strongest residual direction; no-go now

Clients secret-share publication/query embeddings among volunteer compute members.
An established actively secure protocol performs a full 10,000-publication scan
and oblivious result selection. Only authorized results reach clients; publication
bodies remain encrypted. Durable admission and publication acknowledgements retain
their protected histories.

**Assumptions:** a selected protocol must tolerate the actual malicious/offline
combination. Four participating members with at most one Byzantine leave three
honest participants, but that count alone is no protocol proof. Malicious inputs,
output leakage, adaptive queries, share refresh and recovery must be covered.
Recovery must not reconstruct the corpus at an untrusted participant.

**Path:** current policy/admission → protected share delivery → interactive secure
scan/selection → required durable mutation commits → encrypted result return.
Compute rounds add dependencies; storage receipts cannot replace them.

**Cost/falsifiers:** raw arithmetic scales with 10,000 times embedding dimension;
secure comparison, selection and authentication add costs not yet bounded by a
selected implementation. Reject on unauthorized input/output leakage, K5 attack
failure, or joint K1/K7 overrun. [SANNS](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec20-chen-hao.pdf)
provides a semi-honest two-party reference; its introduction and conclusion leave
malicious security and adaptive-query database recovery outside its demonstrated
solution. Its performance cannot qualify this candidate.

Confidential computation also does not hide client activity. Direct shares expose
source addresses; existing transport retains sparse/colluding-observer failures.
These unresolved joint premises stop prototyping now.

### 3. Volunteer hardware-enclave search — reject under current trust assumptions

An attested enclave decrypts embeddings, performs ordinary similarity search and
encrypts results. Volunteers replicate encrypted state and protected histories;
key transfer admits only approved enclave identities.

**Assumptions:** hardware isolation, attestation roots, approved code and rollback
protection become trusted. These additions are unapproved. Malicious host control
must be distinguished from enclave compromise. Attestation freshness, revocation
and recovery dependencies need mapping; neither permanent cloud dependence nor
sufficient offline verification is assumed.

**Path:** necessary attestation/key establishment → protected request route →
enclave search → admission/history commits → encrypted response. Recovery adds
validated key/state transfer. Ordinary scan costs replace interactive secure
arithmetic, but reference memory, energy and timing remain unmeasured.

**Falsifiers:** compromise of the sole decrypting enclave is in scope; rollback
allows stale authorization; recovery requires an unavailable service; or unchanged
transport fails K6. Replication does not repair exposed plaintext.

## Shared dependency and cost findings

The warm, unqueued guarded-spend branch has approximately seven sequential
RTT-equivalents: coordinator policy plus observer-history read (2); witness RPC
including nested policy/history observation and witness-history append (4); final
atomic certificate/spend append (1). This conditional decomposition excludes client
route, handshakes, embedding, wallet work, disk and retries; it is not a measured
latency bound. Reviewed sources: [verifier](../../packages/relay/src/configured-admission-verifier.ts),
[roles](../../packages/relay/src/protected-admission-roles.ts),
[observer](../../packages/relay/src/protected-policy-observer.ts), and
[history](../../packages/relay/src/protected-admission-history.ts).

The diagnostic deployment has 55 logical history copies: 25 policy and 30
admission copies, on two five-endpoint pools sharing one host/process. These are
not 55 independent devices. The [availability decision](v0.4-feasibility-decision.md)
shows two independent 4-of-5 cohorts at individual availability 0.9 yield only
84.372% joint availability. Complete role placement, correlated outages and the
14-day independent pilot remain required.

| Mode / quantity | Evidence or bound; limitation |
| --- | --- |
| Concurrent diagnostic traffic | 4,741,389 peer-TCP bytes / 5 completions = 0.90435 MiB/completion, including the failed attempt. |
| Full lifecycle diagnostic | 15,395,952 / 13 nominal completions = 1.12944 MiB/completion; includes initialization, replay and fault work, not qualified nominal traffic. |
| 10,000 × 768 FP32 embeddings | 30,720,000 bytes = 29.296875 MiB raw; full additive shares with 32-bit field-encoded coordinates have this volume per party; query share 3,072 bytes. This does not establish preservation of FP32 semantics or a secure-protocol performance bound. |
| Sparse current policy | Zero additional idle privacy bytes; no guaranteed activity hiding. |
| Recovery | Requires retained state, authenticated usable quorum and transfer costs; no qualifying measured total. |

TCP-stream counts omit IP/TCP headers, ACKs, retransmissions, final cleanup, local
HTTP and in-process issuance. Physical incremental energy and sustained reference
memory remain evidence gaps.

## Exact prerequisite decisions; stop here

Subsequent owner decision: **design evaluation** of the quiet-traffic rule is now
authorized within the original cumulative quota ceiling. The
[scheduled-traffic envelope and offline screen](v0.4-scheduled-traffic-envelope.md)
continue candidate 2 without adding another architecture. The owner-approved
requirements above settle the direction for transport trust, outputs and bounded
issuance. Their security evidence, independence/eligibility policies, protocol
selection and production-activation prerequisites remain open.

The [quiet policy](v0.4-quiet-period-decision.md) permits a quiet client link with
no other traffic and no guaranteed indistinguishable schedule. An isolated
delivered operation is observable there. This is a counterexample to the present
configurations, not a universal impossibility result. Incidental maintenance and
local holds do not close it. [Loopix](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf)
uses cover/mixing and its own honest-provider/genuine-user assumptions; those
cannot be silently imported.

The original prerequisite decisions were:

1. Permit **design evaluation** of traffic-independent client scheduling, changing
   the zero-additional-idle-privacy policy, with a numerical sparse-mode byte/power
   envelope. Retain K1–K8; activation still requires validation.
2. Fix the permitted observer/compute collusion boundary and authorized output
   access, and identify independently testable eligibility evidence. Any departure
   from current trust assumptions requires explicit approval.
3. Authorize a new bounded continuation on these same blocker/design IDs, only
   after those premises are settled; this study authorizes no further alternatives.

The approvals recorded above do not substitute for the unresolved evidence or
authorize a new study allowance. Preserve these blocker/design IDs when proposing
the next bounded continuation.

For scale only: fixed 26,708-byte exchanges every T seconds, uniform arrivals and
fixed remaining delay D require 0.95T + D ≤8 seconds. Even D=0 implies T≤8.421
seconds and at least 274.024 decimal MB/client-day of payload. This illustrative
schedule excludes other links and is neither a security lower bound nor an AUC
claim. No schedule or resource increase is selected here.
