# Competing successor recoveries: agreement decision

Status: **bounded executable design evaluation, 2026-10-15**. The installed
[one-shot reservation](v0.4-history-checkpoint-successor.md) is unchanged and can
still block after competing proposals. This evaluation selects a candidate
replacement; it does not activate a writer or reopen lost admission history.

## Decision

Develop an explicit view-change protocol for one successor slot with **five
pinned volunteers and four acknowledgements per phase**. A view orders recovery
attempts for the same predecessor/successor pair; it is not a new writer
generation. Separate tentative preparation from final confirmation. Require a
four-member preparation certificate before issuing any confirmation. Changing
views must carry verifiable prepared evidence in four durable, current-view
reports. Never infer that a decision does not exist because its replies were lost.

This is a proposed replacement protocol, not permission to clear the reservations
already stored by the current API. Keep those records and their tests intact.
Wire formats, persistence, payload transfer and a safe transition from existing
experimental state need their own implementation and verification.

## Why simpler fixes are insufficient

1. **One irreversible vote:** two honest members choose A, two choose B, and a
   dishonest fifth signs both. Each candidate has three votes forever.
2. **Reset on timeout:** A may already have four signatures even if the owner
   has received none. Clearing votes can let B obtain four too; delayed A replies
   then expose the contradiction. Wall-clock expiry cannot revoke a certificate.
3. **Inspect four old votes and pick a majority:** the same reports can conceal
   different completed certificates. Consider reports `0:A, 1:A, 2:B, 3:B` with
   member 4 unavailable:

   | Possible world | Dishonest member | Unqueried honest member 4 | Hidden certificate |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | A | 3, which also signed A | A | A from 0, 1, 3, 4 |
   | B | 0, which also signed B | B | B from 0, 2, 3, 4 |

   Every visible honest statement is consistent with either world. The four
   reports alone cannot authorize discarding A or B. This is a counterexample to
   that reset rule, not an impossibility theorem for every recovery protocol.

[Paxos Made Simple, sections 2.2 and 2.4](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/paxos-simple.pdf)
explains why permanent single votes can split and why progress needs a stable
proposer. Its crash-failure assumptions do not cover a volunteer that lies about
its accepted state. A signed claim by that volunteer is not a quorum proof.

The candidate uses the prepared-evidence/view-change structure described in
[PBFT, sections 4.2–4.5](https://static.usenix.org/publications/library/proceedings/osdi99/full_papers/castro/castro_html/node4.html):
carry prepared certificates into a later view and verify the new selection at
each member. We adapt this to one slot and four-of-five quorums. We do not
implement PBFT's replicated service, leader election, checkpoints or full proof.

The [HotStuff paper, section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.05069) also exposes
progress problems when a new leader uses only a highest certificate and some
members retain unseen locks. For five volunteers, this candidate keeps all four
view-change reports. It is not the simplified two-phase HotStuff variant and
does not claim optimistic responsiveness or unconditional progress.

## Candidate state machine

The two values A and B in the evaluator represent different owner-valid successor
proposals for the **same slot**. A proposal identity is stable across views.
Changing a retry nonce must not change the value being carried forward.

1. **Promise a view.** Atomically persist the higher view and a report of the
   highest preparation certificate retained before an earlier confirmation.
   The report precedes any signature. After promising, reject older prepare and
   confirm requests. Retries of the same view reuse its recorded report.
2. **Validate the proposal.** Require four distinct reports for that view. Every
   attached prepared proof must have four authentic, matching preparation votes
   for this slot and an earlier view. Select the value of the highest-view proof;
   if no report contains a proof, a valid candidate may be proposed. Every voter
   checks this calculation independently. A lone claim of a higher view is
   insufficient, and conflicting highest proofs require refusal.
3. **Prepare once per view.** Check the full selected history and proposal, then
   durably record one prepare vote. Multiple coordinators may race, but a member
   never prepares two different values in the same view. Four prepare votes form
   a prepared certificate. A prepared certificate alone is not final authority.
4. **Retain the proof, then confirm.** Require the matching prepared certificate,
   the member's own preparation and the still-current view. Persist the complete
   proof before emitting confirmation. Four confirmations decide that value,
   even if the owner never receives them. Future view reports must preserve the
   proof behind those confirmations.
5. **Retry through evidence.** A later view can replace unconfirmed tentative
   choices using its four-report proof. It must carry a previously confirmable
   value forward. It cannot delete older signatures or declare them invalid by
   timeout. Final activation remains a separate protocol step.

The implementation will need distinct message domains for these phases, exact
context/root/generation/value binding, durable view allocation, fresh challenges,
bounds on proofs and retries, and recovery of the selected proposal's bytes.
Old `sealed` receipts cannot be relabeled as tentative preparation votes.

### Safety intuition

Two four-member quorums intersect in at least three members, at least two of them
honest under the one-Byzantine assumption. A final certificate therefore leaves
prepared evidence at honest members that a later four-report collection must
intersect. They either report that evidence or had already promised the later
view and cannot issue the old confirmation. This includes certificates assembled
from delayed replies after another attempt starts. The model explores these
orderings; the intersection argument alone is not a complete implementation proof.

### Conditions for progress

The progress check assumes a stable owner attempt, four honest volunteers online,
both candidate payloads available and admissible, and no later competing view
interrupting completion. The fifth volunteer is silent during that continuation.
It is a conditional check, not an election algorithm or an Internet availability
claim. Endless competing attempts, fewer available volunteers, damaged durable
state, capacity refusal and unavailable payloads can still stop recovery.

Agreement does not establish that selecting/restoring an actual spend history is
live. The evaluator abstracts both candidates as complete and admissible at every
honest member. Different partial tails, newly discovered denials and replica
capacity can invalidate that assumption. Integration must preserve all required
history while following a carried proposal; it cannot discard a longer retained
tail to make a consensus vote succeed. Cross-generation ancestry and semantic
restore validation remain open.


source hashes, source base, assumptions, counts and replayable counterexamples.
The evaluator reads no user histories or keys and opens no network connections.

The nine model tests also replay both counterexamples, finish a tentative two/two
split, carry an unseen decision through a third view, reject insufficient reports
and invented proofs, and distinguish an exhausted search from a state-budget
cutoff. TypeScript compilation and all nine tests pass locally. The prior
[reservation CI](https://github.com/Planetary-e/resonance/actions/runs/38711164650)
passed audit, compilation, tests and quick evaluation; it predates this evaluator.

### Exact limits

- Enumeration interleaves promise, prepare and confirmation deliveries, including
  skipped views, competing values, late messages and certificates unknown to the
  owner. It explores only two views and one slot. The third-view continuations
  are scheduled honest completions, not arbitrary three-view exploration.
- Four honest identities are interchangeable. Symmetry reduction permutes each
  member's complete recorded history together; it does not merge different
  views, values or retained proofs. Trace member numbers identify positions in
  that normalized state, not persistent production peer identities.
- The Byzantine member may sign both values, hide evidence, or replay any
  genuinely formable prepared certificate. It cannot forge honest signatures.
  Signature verification, payload validity and complete ciphertext retention
  are assumptions, not executed cryptography or network/storage tests here.
- All honest transitions are atomically durable before messages escape. Restart
  is therefore a no-op on this model state. Real fsync/rename failures, process
  death, rollback and persistence migration need new integration tests.
- Old votes remain available to the adversary indefinitely. The evaluator counts
  possible certificates, not only those delivered to an honest coordinator.
- A fixed state budget reports truncation explicitly. Zero conflicts in a
  truncated search must never be reported as exhaustive validation. This result
  is bounded evidence, not a formal proof for arbitrarily many recoveries.

## Implementation boundary

Next: implement separately versioned, signed view reports and prepared/final
certificates with durable barriers, then test the same adversarial traces using
real crypto, sockets and interrupted persistence. Compose that agreement layer
with complete-history selection/write-back before implementing activation.
Keep the current API fail-closed and preserve its existing reservations until a
reviewed transition is available. Do not migrate damaged histories by creating a
fresh empty slot, and do not expose a reset operation as a workaround.
