# RSO5 recovery architecture — DEC-002 design exploration

**Status: RSO5 REJECTED after complete closure accounting; design-only evidence for
independent R1 review, not implementation authorization or acceptance.** FEAT-001 design acceptance would not pass EVL-003/004/005/015 or any
K gate. R1/R2 and K1–K8 remain open. No threshold, trust, fixture or production
integration change is proposed.

## 1. Invariants and comparison scope

The model preserves exact original business objects, signatures, reducers, rights,
denials and uncertainty; five pinned members/quorum four/at most one Byzantine;
staged fences/floors/locks/replay; role-private issuer/wallet state; complete
prospective funding before promises; and all C/L/D/P/F/W/deadline limits.

Limits remain C=262,144, L/P=65,536, D=524,288, F=32,768, operation
W=4,718,592, cumulative 256 MiB, aggregate 2 GiB/512 entries/1 MiB metadata and
3 s/30 s. No per-participant work reset is allowed.

### Established approaches compared

1. **Immutable append-only log with signed checkpoints.** Content-addressed frames
   are never rewritten. A checkpoint commits only to ordered exact frame identities
   and its previous checkpoint. This gives deterministic replay and makes pending
   frames harmless until referenced. Merkle-tree transparency is related established
   practice for append-only inclusion/consistency evidence; this design uses a direct
   ordered checkpoint because only 72 entries exist and no logarithmic proof is
   needed. Source: [RFC 6962, Certificate Transparency](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962.html),
   accessed 2026-10-25.
2. **Stable snapshot + delta compaction.** Cold state can use a snapshot and lagging
   state a suffix. RSO4 showed that full-snapshot replacement needs a physically
   funded pending generation for mixed-crash availability. Its complete model needs
   C=299,703 B and is rejected. Snapshot+suffix is useful only as a transfer pattern,
   not as a reason to rewrite a catalog.
3. **Snapshot/state transfer plus log catch-up.** Raft's InstallSnapshot transfers a
   cold base before continuing log replication. RSO5 uses the same cold-versus-suffix
   distinction for selected frames/checkpoints, but separately authenticates original
   scopes, private scope keys and exact original business evidence; consensus state
   transfer alone does not provide role-private proof recovery. Source:
   [Ongaro and Ousterhout, *In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm*](https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf),
   accessed 2026-10-25.

**Selected for the only detailed forecast:** immutable self-contained frames with
incremental signed checkpoints and compact scope-key delegation. No second candidate
is advanced.

## 2. Why this is not RSO3 or RSO4

RSO3 wraps every proof for every recipient/transition. RSO4 replaces those capsules
with scope catalogs but requires snapshot/delta consistency and pending full catalog
generations. RSO5 has **no catalog, compaction, per-proof recipient capsule, durable
signed range or second catalog generation**.

Each original-signer frame itself binds original scope/proof identity, a per-proof
DEK wrapped under one scope-key version, and ciphertext. A global protected-owner
append-only checkpoint binds ordered frame identities to a previous checkpoint.
Frames append before checkpoint publication. Old frames/checkpoints are never
rewritten, and unreferenced frames cannot affect business state.

## 3. Identities and standard cryptography

For original owner `O`, genesis `G`, original configuration `Cfg` and context `X`:

```
scopeId = SHA256("resonance:rso5:scope:v1\n" || G || Cfg || X || O)
proofId = SHA256(exact original plaintext)
frameDigest = SHA256(complete RSO5F001 frame)
```

Each scope has independent random 32-byte keys per key version; each proof has an
independent random 32-byte DEK. AES-256-GCM encrypts plaintext and wraps the DEK.
Delegation uses locked `@hpke/core` 1.9.0 P-256 DHKEM/HKDF-SHA256/AES-GCM. Ed25519
keys are never converted to ECDH. Recipient P-256 keys are separately enrolled and
signed by the recipient's Ed25519 key.

A scope key exposes only that original scope, not an arena, other scope, journal,
issuer/wallet or observer state. Original business signatures remain on their exact
original objects; recovery frames are new original-owner recovery signatures, not
replacement business signatures.

## 4. Binary records and domain separation

All integers are big-endian; versions, counts, lengths, overflow and trailing data
are strict. Fresh RSO5 records have no RSO2/RSO3/RSO4 migration claim.

### 4.1 Self-contained immutable frame `RSO5F001`

```
magic                 8
kind                  1
scopeKeyVersion       4
ciphertextLength      4    plaintext length + 28
scopeId              32
proofId              32
originalOwner        32
wrappedDekNonce      12
wrappedDek           32    AES-256-GCM(DEK), under scope key
wrappedDekTag        16
plaintextNonce       12
ciphertext            N    AES-256-GCM(original plaintext), under proof DEK
tag                  16
signature            64    original owner
```

AAD is `"resonance:rso5:frame:v1\n" || kind || scopeKeyVersion ||
ciphertextLength || scopeId || proofId || originalOwner`. The signature covers the
contiguous record through `tag`. Frame overhead is 265 B plus a 256-byte immutable
archive header. Full scope configuration is authenticated through `scopeId`, the
historical signer/configuration chain and delegation, not duplicated per frame.

### 4.2 Incremental signed checkpoint `RSO5H001`

```
magic                 8
layoutVersion         4
sequence              8
previousHead         32
entryCount            4
headDigest           32
signer               32
signature            64
entries               M    ordered exact frame identities
```

Each entry is 101 bytes:

```
scopeId[32] || proofId[32] || frameDigest[32] || kind[1] || frameBytes[4]
```

A regular checkpoint with three entries is 487 B. Bootstrap with 72 entries is
7,456 B. Checkpoints live in L/D/P, not C. A checkpoint is valid only when its exact
previous head, ordered entries and every referenced frame are present, authenticated
and durable. It attests availability/order, not business truth.

The global checkpoint signer must be the protected owner authorized by the executed
journal configuration. For imported original scopes it may checkpoint an exact frame
only after original signature/scope-chain validation and four-copy availability. It
does not become an original-scope signer and cannot mint business evidence.

### 4.3 Delegation bundle `RSO5D001`

One bundle per recipient/handoff contains scope keys, never per-proof capsules:

```
magic                 8
proposal             32
exporter             32
recipient            32
wrappingKeyId        32
scopeEntryCount       4
ciphertextLength      4
reserved              8    zero
enc                  65
ciphertext            M    includes AEAD tag
signature            64    current exporter
```

Each scope-key entry is 68 bytes:

```
scopeId[32] || scopeKeyVersion[4] || scopeKey[32]
```

Bundle physical size is `553 + 68*entryCount`. Closure prices two key versions for
each of ten scopes in each of three bundles: initial self, first transfer and
returning transfer. The exporter must be in `oldSet`, recipient/enrollment in
`newSet`, with authenticated prior delegation/current key, original configuration
chain and private decryption. Original-owner countersignature is not required when
all of those checks pass; the predecessor need not be online.

## 5. Log, checkpoint and crash semantics

### Append and publication

1. Preflight every frame/checkpoint/delegation/journal/policy/activation/head/failed
   extent before effects.
2. Append exact immutable frames and fsync them. Unknown or conflicting identities
   refuse; exact replay appends nothing.
3. Obtain four matching frame acknowledgments.
4. Append and replicate the incremental checkpoint that references those frames in
   deterministic execution order.
5. After four checkpoint copies and local install, advance the journal/proof root.

New unreferenced frames are orphans: they consume declared C if retained, but have no
business effect and cannot satisfy a proof root. Prospective preflight rejects
undeclared orphans. Convergence appends deterministic missing frames only; it never
reorders or rewrites an existing frame.

### Mixed checkpoint crashes

| Matching new checkpoint copies | Required result |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Previous checkpoint and every prior frame remain authoritative/intact. |
| 1 | Previous checkpoint remains authoritative; one new checkpoint is pending only. |
| 3 | Previous checkpoint remains authoritative; three new checkpoints are pending only. |
| 4 | New checkpoint is complete and may install locally; previous frames/checkpoints remain retained. |

A partial frame/checkpoint tail is retained in F and is never available. If complete
failed bytes plus existing F exceeds 32,768, repair/replacement refuses before
modifying the survivor. There is no hidden second catalog generation or mutable
checkpoint overwrite.

### Forks and equivocation

For one `(layoutVersion, sequence)`, different `previousHead` or `headDigest` is a
fork and refuses. A checkpoint with an unknown previous head, missing prior frames,
reordered identities or unauthorized signer is not a convergence candidate. A higher
sequence alone cannot override the selected durable proof root. Byzantine replies
are grouped by exact requested frame/checkpoint digest, and metadata-only duplicates
are checked before payload reads.

### Retirement and recipient recovery

Before old freeze, each successor owns every selected frame and its delegation with
four matching copies. Original frame/checkpoint signatures and the historical signer
chain authenticate imported scopes. No predecessor storage is needed afterward.

On recipient loss/reopen, fetch the exact delegation and checkpoint suffix, privately
unwrap scope keys, then per-proof DEKs and plaintext. Missing/corrupt frames refuse
until exact copies are repaired. Replica replies contain only ciphertext and signed
metadata, never replica-plaintext capability.

## 6. Cold, returning and new-view transfer

Cold disjoint import sends every selected frame, checkpoint suffix and delegation;
private scope/proof/plaintext validation precedes reservation. Returning and
new-view transfers first compare exact proof roots/checkpoint heads and send only
missing frames/suffix/delegations. Four matching copies of every selected frame and
delegation remain mandatory even when their payloads are already present.

Scope rotation affects only new frames: old frames remain wrapped under their old
key version and current recipients receive old and new keys. A removed recipient
gets no new bundle and is guaranteed only plaintext already obtained; there is no
retroactive erasure claim. Fresh-policy/current-key checks reject stale delegation
before service.

Incremental evidence indexes may decode only new and issuer/spend-relevant proofs,
but must reproduce full original reducers for every conflict, denial, split and
retry. An index is an optimization only, never alternative business evidence.

## 7. Exact physical closure

Inventory is 72 proofs/215,344 B across up to ten scopes. C contains immutable
frames/archive headers and three delegations. Checkpoints, journals and pending heads
are explicitly outside C in L/D/P.

```
frame contents = 215344 + 72*265             = 234,424
archive headers = 72*256                     =  18,432
delegations    = 3*(553 + 2*68*10)           =   5,739
                                                 ---------
C total                                         258,595
C limit                                         262,144
headroom                                          3,549
```

The two key versions per scope are included for rotation. There is no catalog,
compaction, pending catalog, per-proof capsule, pack manifest or durable signed
range. No additional scope/proof/delegation is silently admitted.

Checkpoint bounds are 7,456 B bootstrap and 487 B regular, fitting each 16,384 B D
slot and L/P=65,536. Pending heads are explicitly funded in P. Existing L/D/P journal
and policy/activation checks remain unchanged. F refusal is required when complete
failed extents do not fit 32,768.

Checkpoints/delegations add no reservation components. Existing aggregate
2 GiB/512/1 MiB arithmetic is unchanged; prior prospective accounting was
2,042,919,814 B / 444 entries, leaving 104,563,834 B and 68 entries.

## 8. Bottom-up work forecast and cumulative rejection

These are named-record/copy/validation/write forecasts, not percentage reductions or
acceptance results. Checkpoint creation and suffix transfer are included inside each
relevant operation; they are not omitted into a separate optimistic line item.

### 8.1 Primitives

Let `A=265`, `H=256`, `P=72`, `B=215344`, `J=65536` and
`Rref=6*(192+64)=1,536` per proof for signed reference metadata across six
participants. A maximum journal read/write is `9J+11*284=592,948`. Policy/activation
shape construction costs `2*(2*16384+2*12288)=114,688`.

One full ten-scope/two-key-version delegation costs:

```
D = 2*(2*68*10) + 11*(553 + 2*68*10) = 23,763 B
```

Creation charges `15b + 11*A` per proof: original input/ciphertext/direct check and
comparison, one frame allocation, five replica input buffers and six writes.
Existing-frame import charges `14b + 12*A`: source/read/decrypt/compare, five
replica buffers and six writes. Every frame also costs `6H` archive work.

A three-entry checkpoint costs `11*(184+3*101) = 5,357` across signature/
validation/input/write materializations. The complete 72-entry bootstrap checkpoint
costs `11*(184+72*101) = 82,016`; a 30-entry returning suffix costs
`11*(184+30*101) = 35,354`.

## 9. Narrow falsifier retained for a later separate decision

The following is a falsifier for any future independently reviewed alternative, not
authorization to implement RSO5: a complete 72-proof/ten-scope/two-key-version cold
bootstrap must keep C<=262,144 and measured aggregate W<=4,718,592, privately recover
after predecessor shutdown, and preserve the previous checkpoint at 0/1/3/4 copies.
Any replica plaintext, orphan business effect or accepted checkpoint fork stops the
slice. A primitive pass would not cure the cumulative failure above and would not
justify transfer integration.

Required later tests include wrong scope/signer/delegation/key version; checkpoint
forks/reordering/missing previous heads; orphan frames; exact replay/conflicts;
0/1/3/4 checkpoint crashes; partial tails/F exhaustion; first cold and returning/
new-view availability; stale delegation and rotation; recipient loss/reopen;
predecessor retirement; missing/corrupt frames; and incremental versus full original
reducers for every conflict/denial/split/retry.

## 10. Verdict and remaining reviewer questions

**RSO5 is REJECTED.** Physical C closes at 258,595 B with 3,549 B headroom, and the
individual operation forecasts are bootstrap 4,249,359 B, first cold reserve
4,278,375 B and returning reserve 1,728,233 B. Complete cumulative work is
270,132,258 B, exceeding 268,435,456 by 1,696,802 B. No implementation is justified.

Questions for a genuinely different future model:

1. Can checkpoint authority and availability/order-only signing be retained without
   the measured cumulative checkpoint/suffix obligations, without weakening
   convergence or four-copy guarantees?
2. Are authenticated consistency proofs necessary in addition to previous-head links,
   and what is their exact physical/work cost?
3. Does two-key-version delegation prevent stale service under every rotation and
   fresh-policy schedule?
4. Is every pending head and failed tail in L/D/P/F included in the actual runtime?
5. Can incremental evidence exactly reproduce full original reducers in every case?
6. What independent real-network, maximum-envelope and 3 s/30 s evidence is required?
   This design supplies no timing, 100-transition, EVL or K-gate result.

The correct response to another incomplete model is REVISE/REJECT, not fitting
constants or implementing the rejected architecture. No acceptance is claimed.

## 11. Independent R1 verdict

Independent review returned **CONFIRM REJECTION**. It recomputed C=258,595 B,
bootstrap=4,249,359 B, cold reserve=4,278,375 B, returning reserve=1,728,233 B and
cumulative work=270,132,258 B. The excess over 256 MiB remains 1,696,802 B. The
cold-reserve frame line was corrected from 3,354,512 to 3,354,368 B; the published
total had already used the correct value, so no total changed.

The reviewer found the physical/crash model coherent: checkpoint signatures attest
only availability/order after executed-journal authorization, not original business
truth; previous-head links require executed proof roots and conflict checks rather
than highest-sequence selection; orphan frames have no business effect and remain
charged; frames precede checkpoints and journal advancement requires four checkpoint
copies plus local installation. Rotation preserves old-frame key versions, requires
current recipients to hold old and new keys, and refuses an unfunded third version.
Cold import includes all selected frames/suffixes/delegations; returning/new-view
references require exact availability and suffix catch-up. Original reducers remain
authoritative and incremental evidence must prove identical conflict/denial/split/
retry outcomes.

RFC 6962 and Raft support only the bounded append-only/checkpoint and snapshot+
suffix patterns; neither establishes consensus or role-private recovery here.
Removing checkpoint work, recovery copies or operation phases, per-participant resets
or omitting returning work is forbidden. No implementation, campaign, threshold
change or R1/R2/K-gate acceptance is justified.
